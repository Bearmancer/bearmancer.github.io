The company

  • Amway — the soap company whose products the distributorships move; a portmanteau of "American Way"; founded by Richard DeVos and Jay Van Andel, two men who had previously sold for Nutrilite. In the entry stories, Amway itself looks like the minor party — "just the corporation that manufactures the soap".
  • Nutrilite — the organization DeVos and Van Andel sold for before Amway; the video describes it as generally regarded as the first multi-level marketing company (claim checked in lesson 0001: the multi-level system dates to 1945, Mytinger & Casselberry's national distributorship of Nutrilite).
  • Ja-Ri Corporation — the company DeVos and Van Andel actually formed in 1949 (name from their first names), later the vehicle for their Nutrilite distributorship; merged into Amway Corporation in 1964. This is the 1949 the video half-attributes to Amway itself.
  • The American Way Association — incorporated April 1959; the direct ancestor of the Amway name ("Amway" = short for "American Way"). Amway Sales and Services Corporations follow in September 1959. Canonical founding date used here: 9 November 1959.
  • Direct Distributor — the top tier named in the Wisconsin record: about 200 people out of ~20,000 Wisconsin distributorships, i.e. the "top 1%" of the video's shorthand; their 1979–1980 tax returns produced the $918 average net loss.
  • Richard DeVos — co-founder; with Van Andel, a conservative evangelical Calvinist whose worldview, per the video, "shaped the entire Amway universe".
  • Jay Van Andel — co-founder; same background and role.

The roles & structure

  • Distributor / IBO ("independent business owner") — anyone who accepts the invitation and joins; Amway's own term is IBO, the general English term is distributor. The video's point: the jargon makes a buyer into a "business owner".
  • Line of sponsorship — the chain of recruiters a distributor joins.
  • Upline — the person who recruited you (for the viewer: "your friend"); product flows down this relationship, money and "cut" flow up it.
  • Downline — the people you recruit, and their recruits; the thing you are told will make you money.
  • Multi-level marketing (MLM) — the business form where income comes primarily from recruiting further sellers rather than from retail customers; the video treats Amway as its archetype.

The practice & vocabulary

  • "Showing the plan" / "drawing circles" — the classic recruitment meeting: butcher paper, markers, circles standing for recruits and their recruits; Amway is often named only at the very end.
  • Tools — the motivational materials and events sold to distributors, above all cassette tapes; the subject of the whole video. Chapter 2 only says the weekly pickup at your friend's house includes "boxes of soap and some other stuff, which becomes important later". Nothing more is defined yet.
  • The Wisconsin study (as told in chapter 2) — "in 1980, the Wisconsin Attorney General crunched the numbers of the top 1% of Amway distributors in that state"; nearly all lost money net of operating costs; average loss put at $900 (claim checked in lesson 0001).
