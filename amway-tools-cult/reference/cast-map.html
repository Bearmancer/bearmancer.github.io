Cast & relationship map — as of chapter 4

How to read the colours

Every line type is a different kind of relationship, not a different chapter. Line colour + style are duplicated in the legend below the diagram, and every line is also labelled in place. Uncertain links (unknown identities, declined proposals) are dashed and grey.

Legend

  • Green, solid — Amway links. Co-founding, and the sponsorship (upline → downline) genealogy: DeVos → Hansen; Yager → Renard; Yager → Conigliaro → Britt.
  • Blue, solid — the tape business. Manufacturing and wholesale supply of the tools: Britt produces, Yager distributes through his downline on a sweetheart deal.
  • Amber, dashed — motivational lineage. Who inspired what: Hill → Nightingale → the record that reaches Yager; Carnegie and Peale are the same self-help wave.
  • Purple, dashed — family. The one household link the chapter gives: Birdie Yager.
  • Teal, dotted — testimony. Who tells the story: Wead is the source of the Fontainebleau founding account (and a kingpin himself).
  • Red, dashed — separate ownership. Amway Corporation and the tools businesses are different firms with different owners; the relationship is "historically acrimonious" (ch3).
  • Grey, dashed — unknown or declined. The unrecorded sponsor, the nameless young man, and the $50,000 proposal Yager turned down.

Who is who — the roster

The company side

  • Rich DeVos & Jay Van Andel — Amway's co-founders (1959). In this chapter DeVos also has a walk-on: he reportedly sponsored Fred Hansen, "one of the original Amway distributors", back in the '50s.
  • Amway Corporation — the soap-and-products company. Important for the map because it is not the tools business: separate owners, separate profits (ch3's two-pyramid distinction).

The chain that matters

  • Dexter Yager — joined Amway in 1964, five years into the company's life; by his own hagiography a born salesman (sixth-grade soda racket), by the record a car and beer salesman, twice poor, briefly a heavy drinker. What his own story skipped: he made no real money in Amway, and was job-hunting by about 1967. He moved to Charlotte in 1969 with Birdie and the children — Charlotte becomes the story's centre stage.
  • Fred Hansen — early distributor (sponsored by DeVos in the '50s); possibly the man who lent Yager the Nightingale record. The video keeps this uncertain and so does this map.
  • Tony Renard — sponsored by Yager, fall 1966; buys a second-hand tape recorder from him and starts duplicating motivational tapes — by the late '60s they move through Yager's downline, not yet as a profit centre.
  • Dominic Conigliaro — Yager's downline; sponsors Bill Britt into Amway in 1970. Britt: Korean War army service, minor municipal offices around Charlotte, "virtually a black box" of a biography — and the man who, at a coffee-shop meeting in the Fontainebleau Hotel, heard a $50,000 tape-plant pitch that Yager declined and answered "I'll do it."
  • ? The nameless young man — the pitch's originator, left unnamed by the chapter's source; identity genuinely unknown (Wead himself is considered and set aside as too young/too unknown-to-Yager at that date).

Around the founders

  • Birdie Yager — Dexter's wife; four children at the Charlotte move, seven eventually.
  • Doug Wead — a tools kingpin who is also this chapter's key source: the Fontainebleau story is his written account. The video promises a fuller profile later.
  • Betty Joe Renfro — wife of a tools kingpin; already on stage in ch3 with the "tape of the day, or the tape of the hour" line. Her husband is not yet named.

The lineage (the map's left-hand roots)

  • Earl Nightingale, "The Strangest Secret" (1956) — the first motivational record, and the model for the whole duplication practice.
  • Napoleon Hill (Think and Grow Rich, 1937) — Nightingale's inspiration; the video adds that Hill was a con man riding the first self-help wave.
  • Dale Carnegie & Norman Vincent Peale — the same 1930s–50s wave; the chapter's point is that the tools' content was recycled from an existing tradition, not invented for Amway.

Context that is deliberately not drawn

  • Holiday Magic and Koscot Interplanetary — two parallel proto-MLMs also growing motivational divisions in the late '60s. No personal links to these founders on the record yet, so no line.
  • Amway's own small motivational operation — exists at this date, separate from Britt/Yager's venture.
  • Cultural walk-ons (Manson, the Trumps) are in the treatise, not the diagram — they are not actors in this story.
