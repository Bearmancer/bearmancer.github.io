Reference — who is who, and who is wired to whom

Cast & relationship map — as of chapter 7

Coverage: every player the video has introduced through chapter 7 ("The Battle With Amway", ends 1:06:28) · nothing from chapter 8 or later · diagram v4: one colour per relation, solid lines only, no edge crosses a name box · names are canonical (see glossary)

How to read the colours

Each kind of relationship has its own colour — that is the only difference between lines: every line is solid and the same weight. The colours are this diagram's own key, defined by the legend below (not a universal scheme; they stay stable across chapters of this same map). A link the video's own fact-check flagged as disputed keeps a colour of its own rather than a different line style. The legend below the diagram is complete for this map, and every line is labelled in place.

Legend

  • Green — founding & ownership. Who created, bankrolled or co-created the company: DeVos and Van Andel → Amway; Patrick → Holiday Magic and Leadership Dynamics (plus his stake in Mind Dynamics); Everett → Mind Dynamics; Turner → Koscot and Dare to Be Great; Erhard → EST.
  • Blue — Amway sponsorship, as the record supports it. Upline → downline: Yager → Renard; Yager → Conigliaro → Britt.
  • Grey — a link the video asserts but the record disputes. Hansen's sponsorship: the video says DeVos, the obituary record says the Basses (see lesson 0002).
  • Purple — the tape business. Renard's duplication feeding Yager's downline; Britt manufacturing and supplying tapes wholesale; Hall's late-1982 bid to build a rival tools line by crosslining (the attempt, not a success).
  • Brown — the Fontainebleau proposal. One pitch, two answers: declined by Yager, accepted by Britt (outcomes in the labels).
  • Amber — intellectual lineage. Hill → Nightingale → the duplication practice; Carnegie and Peale are the same self-help wave.
  • Magenta — family. The one household link the chapters give: Birdie Yager.
  • Teal — testimony & authorship. Who tells the story and who wrote it: Wead is the source of the Fontainebleau founding account; Wead was Yeager's ghostwriter on the books pushed as tools (ch6); Wead co-wrote the 1979 Peoples Temple book with Phil Kerns.
  • Red — separation & termination. Amway Corporation and the tools businesses: different owners, "historically acrimonious" (ch3); Amway terminates Leonard Hall's distributorship for crosslining, late 1982 (the video's account — no record trace found).
  • Navy — regulatory enforcement. The FTC moves against all three pyramid sellers: Holiday Magic (Docket 8834 — with state and SEC suits alongside), Koscot (Docket 8888, order 1975), Amway (complaint 1975, decision 1979).
  • Olive — political & ideological ties. DeVos and Van Andel's private 1975 White House visit to Ford (the visit is the video's assertion; no record of its content); Ford speaking at Amway conventions; Yeager bringing candidate Reagan to the 1980 Charlotte rally; Wead as adviser and conduit to the Bush family; Patrick's membership in the John Birch Society; Erhard's involvement in Mind Dynamics.
  • Violet — parallel-model resemblance. The same pyramid-shaped MLM form shared by Amway, Holiday Magic, Koscot and Dare to Be Great — with no personal link claimed between those companies and this cast (the Koscot-distributors-turned-kingpins crossover is tape lore only; see "not drawn").

Who is who — the roster

The company side

  • Rich DeVos & Jay Van Andel — Amway's co-founders (1959). DeVos also has a walk-on in this story: the video says he sponsored Fred Hansen in the '50s; the record shows the Basses did. In 1975 both men privately visit President Ford in the White House — the visit is the video's assertion; what was said there is unknown (ch7).
  • Amway Corporation — the soap-and-products company; not the tools business (separate owners, separate profits — ch3's two-pyramid distinction). Fights the FTC 1975–79 and survives with safe-harbor conditions the suits read as a draw, not an absolution (ch7).
  • The FTC (ch7) — the regulator that killed Holiday Magic and Koscot and then came for "the biggest steer on the ranch": complaint against Amway March 1975, final order May 1979 (In re Amway Corp., 93 F.T.C. 618).

The chain that matters

  • Dexter Yager — joined Amway 1964; the tape business's distribution half. Born 1939 upstate New York (Fulton — the video says Rome; he was raised there); car and beer salesman (Utica Club); moved to Charlotte 1969 with Birdie and the children. Sponsors Renard (1966); declines the $50,000 tape-plant pitch; distributes Britt's tapes through his downlines. Brings candidate Reagan to the 1980 Charlotte Amway rally (ch6).
  • Fred Hansen — "one of the original Amway distributors"; possibly the man who lent Yager the Nightingale record (unverified either way).
  • Tony Renard — sponsored by Yager, Fall 1966; buys a second-hand tape recorder from him and starts duplicating motivational tapes; by the late '60s they move through Yager's downline — "at first, they're not a profit center". This is the practice Britt later scales into mass manufacturing; the chapters record no direct link between Renard and Britt (the video's founding trio is Britt, Yager, and the nameless man). Caveat: the name is unfindable in the accessible record — treat as oral tradition (lesson 0002).
  • Dominic Conigliaro — Yager's downline; sponsors Bill Britt into Amway in 1970. Name spelling varies in the record (Coniguliaro/Congilero).
  • Bill Britt — the tape business's manufacturing half. Army 1949, Korean War officer; municipal management in central North Carolina (the video says "Charlotte area"; the record says Raleigh, Sanford, Goldsboro, Carrboro); joined Amway 1970. At the Fontainebleau coffee shop he answered the nameless man's pitch with "I'll do it."
  • ? The nameless young man — the pitch's originator, unnamed in the chapter's source; identity genuinely unknown (Wead considered and set aside).
  • Leonard Hall (ch7) — a West Coast distributor who "goes rogue" late 1982: seeing the tools money, he poaches distributors up and down the California coast to build a rival tools line of his own — crosslining, "the ultimate sin in the Amway universe" — and Amway terminates his distributorship. The video admits it knows very little about Hall, and no press or court trace of him surfaced: everything in this entry is the video's account, mostly via the Miami Showdown tape.

Around the founders

  • Birdie Yager — Dexter's wife; four children at the Charlotte move, seven eventually.
  • Doug Wead — tools kingpin and the chapter's key source (the Fontainebleau account is his). Chapter 6 profiles him: Yeager's assistant, press agent and ghostwriter (the Reagan biography he wrote is dedicated to Yeager); wired into the Republican establishment through Amway; close to the Bush family under both 41 and 43, then White House staff 1989–90; released secretly taped Bush conversations in 2005; later advised the Pauls. Chameleon source — read with the spin in mind.
  • Betty Jo Renfrow — wife of tools kingpin Rex Renfrow; the "tape of the day, or the tape of the hour" line (ch3). Spelling corrected per the record (captions render "Betty Joe Renfro").
  • Phil Kerns (ch7) — former distributor; author of Fake It 'Til You Make It (1982), the first book-length exposé of Amway and the tools cult. Irony of record: he once co-wrote a book about the Peoples Temple with Doug Wead (Peoples Temple, Peoples Tomb, 1979).

The parallel empires (ch5)

  • William Penn Patrick — founded Holiday Magic Inc. (1964) on the Amway multilevel model; founded Leadership Dynamics (1967), pioneering large-group awareness training; held a financial stake in Mind Dynamics; member of the John Birch Society; died 1973, after which his companies shut down.
  • Alexander Everett — Patrick's mentor; founder of Mind Dynamics, the "sister corporation" of Holiday Magic.
  • Glenn W. Turner — Holiday Magic employee turned founder: Koscot Interplanetary (cosmetics, like Holiday Magic) and Dare to Be Great! (1969), graduated self-motivation courses on cassette, $8,000 for all four levels. Koscot was driven out by the FTC in 1975; Turner went to prison in 1987 for a later pyramid scheme and died 2020.
  • Holiday Magic Inc. — Patrick's cosmetics MLM; prosecuted by California, the SEC and the FTC as an illegal pyramid (FTC Docket 8834).
  • Koscot Interplanetary — Turner's cosmetics MLM (FTC Docket 8888, order 1975). Celebrity footnote: F. Lee Bailey was indicted with Turner in 1973 (hung jury).
  • Leadership Dynamics — Patrick's 1967 large-group-awareness company.
  • Mind Dynamics — Everett's company, Patrick's stake, Erhard's involvement; subject of a 1972 book-length exposé.
  • Dare to Be Great! — Turner's 1969 Orlando motivation-course company; the cassette-and-seminar model the chapter holds up as the tools' forerunner.
  • Werner Erhard & EST — Erhard was involved in Mind Dynamics, then founded Erhard Seminars Training (EST); the human-potential movement's most famous brand, name-checked from The Wiz to Diana Ross fandom.
  • John Birch Society — the far-right "worldwide communist conspiracy" organization; Patrick was a member. The chapter's bridge from motivational cults to far-right politics (with the kingpins' conservative-evangelical-Republican profile flagged as a forward thread).

The politicians (ch6–ch7)

  • Ronald Reagan (ch6) — candidate Reagan speaks at Yeager's massive 1980 Charlotte Amway rally (Wead wrote the campaign biography Reagan in Pursuit of the Presidency, dedicated to Yeager).
  • The Bush family (ch6) — George H. W. Bush (41) and George W. Bush (43): Wead becomes close to the family, serves as religious-right conduit in the White House 1989–90, is cashiered after the 1990 hate-crimes-bill fight, and burns the bridge by releasing secretly taped calls in 2005.
  • Gerald Ford (ch7) — Grand Rapids congressman next door to Ada, accidental president; Amway's "secret weapon" that Holiday and Koscot lacked. DeVos and Van Andel visit him privately in the White House in 1975 — asserted, content unknown. A fixture at Amway conventions thereafter.

The lineage (the map's lower band)

  • Earl Nightingale, "The Strangest Secret" (1956) — the first motivational record and the model for the duplication practice.
  • Napoleon Hill (Think and Grow Rich, 1937) — Nightingale's inspiration; the video's "con man" characterization is substantially supported by investigative journalism.
  • Dale Carnegie & Norman Vincent Peale — the same 1930s–50s wave the tools recycled.

Context that is deliberately not drawn

  • The Koscot-distributors-turned-kingpins crossover — several former Koscot distributors "apparently became tools cult kingpins"; the claim rests on a casual mention on the 1983 Miami Showdown tape. Tape lore, not a drawable link: no names, no lines.
  • The White House deal — whether the 1975 Ford visit produced an explicit or implicit understanding about the FTC. The video itself refuses to pick ("we may never know"); the map draws the visit, not the deal.
  • The unverified numbers — the $30M-a-year Yager figure and the 2%/98% tools-profit split are the video's figures, not the record's (lesson 0003); no line is annotated with them.
  • The Hawaii parallel — "cryptic references" to a second crosslining affair the video says it "couldn't nail down". Unnailed, undrawn.
  • Amway's own small motivational operation — exists at this date, separate from Britt/Yager's venture.
  • Walk-ons — Barry Goldwater (endorsed Wead's 1992 Democratic opponent), Ron and Rand Paul (Wead's later advisees), the January-6 / Iced Earth aside, QAnon, Scientology, F. Lee Bailey and O.J. Simpson, the Manson–Fromme montage figures, Diana Ross and The Wiz: colour in the treatises, not actors in this wiring.
  • Named-but-unopened documents — the Postma memo and the Miami Showdown tape are named as sources the video leans on; their contents belong to later chapters and are not drawn.
  • Cultural walk-ons from earlier chapters (Manson, the Trumps) — in the treatise, not the diagram; they are not actors in this story.
