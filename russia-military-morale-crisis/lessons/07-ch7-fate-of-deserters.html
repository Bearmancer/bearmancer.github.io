The Fate of Deserters

The couple's Black Sea escape is the same flight the RFE/RL odyssey account traces. They describe a Greek pushback to Aydin in Turkey, a beating once the cameras went dark, and a paused deportation order.

Closed Borders and Low Asylum Odds

Germany approves fewer than five percent of Russian deserter claims, per the DW asylum account via Kozlov, a human-rights activist, who attributes the refusals to fear of infiltration. Georgy tells of a border ambush with two killed by a patrol helicopter, and describes phone-record tracking, address checks, and pressure on relatives closing the net around him.

The Unofficial Jail

The jail heard as Rozsypne is the Zaitsevo facility in the Moscow Times secret-jail account — same site, variant name. Inside: sorting for "re-education," killings on the spot, and beatings calibrated to hurt without marking.

"They used tasers, hit him in the stomach, just enough to hurt, but not leave any bruises."

Russia's military tortures deserters' families to force men back, per Meduza's families piece — case reporting; the UN frames the practice as system-wide.

Every Exit Closed in Sequence

  • Europe: pushbacks and single-digit asylum odds.
  • The border: helicopters, phone records, and relatives as leverage.
  • Capture: trial or disappearance, with return to the front as the best case.

Every exit a deserter tries — asylum, the border, capture — closes in turn.
