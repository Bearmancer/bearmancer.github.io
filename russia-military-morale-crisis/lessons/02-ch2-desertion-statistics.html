1. Summary

No official Russian count exists, so the chapter stacks three unofficial numbers. Each needs a different grade: a floor, a relay, and a projection.

3. Narrative

Russia publishes no deserter count, and the narration reads that silence as fear of handing the West a propaganda victory. The secrecy is plausible, but the motive reading stays unverified as stated.

The 28,000 convictions since the invasion began are confirmed as Meduza's reported court tally. Because it counts only soldiers who were caught, it is partially correct as a measure of desertion: a floor, never a total.

The 50,000 figure is confirmed as a relay of the UN estimate through the Novaya Gazeta write-up of the UN findings. The "almost double the convicts" arithmetic is supported, so the doubling claim reads as confirmed arithmetic on relayed inputs.

The 70,000-plus figure is corrected: it is a Frontelligence Insight projection of voluntary departures by year-end 2025, not a counted total. The attached "almost 10 percent of deployed forces" is partially correct arithmetic, contingent on which deployed-force denominator is used.

Two stray corrections close the ledger. Figures of 16,000 and 13,500 sometimes attached to this debate are corrected as misattributed cumulative and all-service counts, per the same 2 UN relay record. And the video's 200,000 for Ukrainian unauthorized absence is corrected to the documented 140,000 to 180,000 range, against the same 3 desertion-data record.

4. Machinery

The chapter works as a ladder: floor, relay, projection.

  • Convictions anchor the floor: only the caught, only the sentenced.
  • The UN relay scales it up: an estimate with methodology attached.
  • The projection extends it forward: a year-end scenario, never a census.

Readers should never collapse the three rungs into one number. Each answers a different question, and only the first is a count.
