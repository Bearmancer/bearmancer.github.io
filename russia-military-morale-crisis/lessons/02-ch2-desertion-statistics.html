Desertion Statistics

Russia publishes no deserter count — read as fear of handing the West a propaganda point.

Convictions: The Floor

28,000 convictions since the invasion began, per Meduza's reported court tally. It counts only soldiers who were caught — a floor for desertion, never a total.

The UN Estimate: The Relay

Over 50,000 Russian soldiers deserted since 2022, per the UN estimate in the Novaya Gazeta write-up of the UN findings — almost double the convicts. Figures of 16,000 and 13,500 sometimes cited in this debate count different things: cumulative and all-service tallies, not the same desertion total.

Frontelligence: The Projection

Over 70,000 voluntary departures by year-end 2025, per the Frontelligence Insight projection — a forward scenario, not a counted total, and "almost 10 percent of deployed forces" shifts with the denominator used. The 200,000 for Ukrainian unauthorized absence sits above the documented 140,000-to-180,000 range.

Floor, Relay, Projection

  • Convictions anchor the floor: only the caught, only the sentenced.
  • The UN estimate scales it up: 50,000 with methodology attached.
  • The projection extends it forward: a year-end scenario, never a census.

The three rungs never collapse into one number. Each answers a different question, and only the first is a count.
