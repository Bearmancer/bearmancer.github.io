Infiltration and Morale

Two-to-three-person sabotage groups slip past Ukrainian lines — small Russian infiltration teams described in reporting on small Russian infiltration groups. Handlers direct them by drone; they lay mines and hunt Ukrainian drone crews.

"A hundred men go in, a handful come out" is an illustration of loss tolerance — a figure of speech, not a count.

The Price of Evasion

Dodging an assault costs fifty thousand to a million rubles; a fake injury certificate runs up to six million — the bribe scale in Meduza's reported extortion economy. The video reads it as the men assigning the danger collecting the money — commanders running the market they feed, a reading Meduza's reporting supports in part. Verstka's "zeroing" accounts add dedicated executioners and snipers for deserters — enforcement stories that frighten whether or not they are policy. The closing menu for the Russian soldier: the meat grinder, the bribe, or death.

A Real Tactic and a Real Market

  • Infiltration as the effective edge: small, deniable, drone-eyed.
  • Evasion as the priced good: safety sold by the men assigning danger.
  • Execution stories as enforcement: believed enough to frighten.

Morale follows the price list. When survival is sold back to the soldier, the army's will problem is structural, not temperamental.
