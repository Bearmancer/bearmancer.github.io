1. Summary

Small groups slip forward while commanders sell safety behind the line. The tactics are corroborated; the atrocity relay is not.

3. Narrative

The two-to-three-person sabotage groups guided past Ukrainian lines are confirmed against reporting on small Russian infiltration groups. Drone-directed handlers, mine-laying, and drone-detection missions stay the source's tactical relay, partially correct where that 1 small-group record corroborates the practice.

The "hundred men in, a handful survive" ratio is an illustrative figure of speech, unverified as a count. It dramatizes real loss tolerance without measuring it.

The bribe figures are confirmed as Meduza's reported extortion economy: fifty thousand to a million rubles to dodge assaults, up to six million for a fake injury certificate. Commanders running the market they feed is the narration's inference on those figures, partially correct against the same 2 reporting.

The Verstka "zeroing" relay, dedicated executioners, and snipers for deserters all sit at allegation level here: the outlet's page is unreachable, so a single-outlet relay cannot rise higher. The closing menu of meat grinder, bribe, or death is editorial rhetoric, unverified as a description of any soldier's real options.

4. Machinery

The chapter works by pairing a real tactic with a real market.

  • Infiltration as the effective edge: small, deniable, drone-eyed.
  • Evasion as the priced good: safety sold by the men assigning danger.
  • Execution stories as the enforcement rumor: believed enough to frighten, sourced too thinly to grade higher than allegation.

Morale follows the price list. When survival is sold back to the soldier, the army's will problem is structural, not temperamental.
