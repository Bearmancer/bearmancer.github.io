Casualties Becoming Unsustainable

The framing here is slanted: its one-directional rounding leans every uncertain figure toward catastrophe. The direction of travel may hold while the numbers do not.

Figures the Record Will Not Carry

  • The three-hundred-fifty-thousand figure is exaggerated against the CSIS range of two-hundred-seventy-five to three-hundred-twenty-five thousand.
  • The three-hundred-sixteen per square kilometer with two-hundred-twenty and one-hundred-eighty-nine has no second source. The usable control record is the September assessment relay of one-hundred-forty-seven point seven seized with net minus twenty and nine-hundred-seventy-two point five six liberated.
  • The one-in-twenty-five and one-in-sixteen rates have no second source.
  • The ninety-five percent figure is a share of strikes rather than a share of casualties.

What Survives

The two-to-one inversion and the wounded-early pattern survive. The projection-versus-count discipline follows the Frontelligence Insight projection lesson from chapter 2, the category discipline follows the UN relay record, and the projection-range reading follows the ten-year rebuild headline precedent.

The casualty arithmetic is separate from the chapter 2 desertion counts of twenty-eight thousand convicted, fifty thousand relayed, and seventy thousand projected. Drone lethality here extends chapter 4 without revising its reframed countdown.

Precision Without Sourcing

Decimal figures perform a certainty the sourcing cannot fund.

  • False precision: exact rates mask missing sources.
  • Category slides: strikes become casualties, projections become counts.
  • Surviving core: inversion and early-return patterns outlast the numbers.
