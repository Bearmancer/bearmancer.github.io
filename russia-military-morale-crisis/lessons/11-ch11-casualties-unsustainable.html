3. Narrative

The framing here is slanted, and that verdict is confirmed by the chapter's one-directional rounding: every uncertain figure leans toward catastrophe. The direction of travel may hold while the numbers do not.

Tier2 verdicts observed 2026-09-29, with corrections applied. The three-hundred-fifty-thousand figure is graded wrong as exaggerated, corrected against the CSIS two-hundred-seventy-five to three-hundred-twenty-five-thousand range. The three-hundred-sixteen per square kilometer with two-hundred-twenty and one-hundred-eighty-nine is unverifiable, with no witness located; the usable control record is the September assessment relay of one-hundred-forty-seven point seven seized with net minus twenty and nine-hundred-seventy-two point five six liberated. The one-in-twenty-five and one-in-sixteen rates stay unverified, and the ninety-five percent figure is corrected as a share of strikes rather than a share of casualties.

Supported: the two-to-one inversion and the wounded-early pattern, which survive the audit. The projection-versus-count discipline follows the Frontelligence Insight projection lesson from chapter 2, the category discipline follows the UN relay record, and the projection-range reading follows the ten-year rebuild headline precedent.

Dedupe against chapters 1 to 7: casualty arithmetic is new here and is not mixed with the chapter 2 desertion counts of twenty-eight thousand convicted, fifty thousand relayed, and seventy thousand projected. Drone lethality here extends chapter 4 without revising its reframed countdown.

4. Machinery

The chapter works by precision theater: decimal figures perform certainty the sourcing cannot fund.

  • False precision: exact rates mask missing witnesses.
  • Category slides: strikes become casualties, projections become counts.
  • Surviving core: inversion and early-return patterns outlast the numbers.
