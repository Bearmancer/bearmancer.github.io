Casualties Becoming Unsustainable

The numbers here lean one way — every uncertain figure rounds toward catastrophe, so the direction of travel and the arithmetic pull apart.

Figures the Course Will Not Carry

  • The three-hundred-fifty-thousand figure runs above the CSIS range of two-hundred-seventy-five to three-hundred-twenty-five thousand.
  • The three-hundred-sixteen per square kilometer, with two-hundred-twenty and one-hundred-eighty-nine, has no parallel in the September assessment relay, which put one-hundred-forty-seven point seven seized, net minus twenty, and nine-hundred-seventy-two point five six liberated.
  • The one-in-twenty-five and one-in-sixteen rates appear in one account only.
  • The ninety-five percent figure is a share of strikes, not a share of casualties.

What Survives

The two-to-one inversion and the wounded-early pattern. The projection-versus-count caution comes from the Frontelligence Insight projection of chapter 2, the category caution from the UN relay record, and the projection-range caution from the ten-year rebuild headline.

The casualty arithmetic stays separate from chapter 2's desertion counts — twenty-eight thousand convicted, fifty thousand relayed, seventy thousand projected — and drone lethality here continues chapter 4 without revising its countdown.

Precision Without Sourcing

Decimal figures claim a certainty the numbers do not earn.

  • Exact rates mask missing totals.
  • Strikes slide into casualties; projections into counts.
  • Inversion and early-return patterns outlast the numbers.
