Life Expectancy and Tactics

The relayed figure gives a recruit twenty to thirty minutes after reaching the battlefield, attributed to the CIA director through a paywalled outlet that could not be reached. The viral "thirty minutes flat" version overstates it. The outline is consistent with open-source drone lethality, but it rests on one relayed remark, not a measured study.

"It begins the moment they move forward, are spotted by a reconnaissance drone, and then become a target for a strike drone."

The reframing is the chapter's load-bearing move: the countdown starts at spotting, not at arrival. The arithmetic behind it is valid as illustration, since forty-eight front updates a day and twenty-one men across a thousand-kilometer line are reductio math, not counts.

The World War I fifteen-minute and Normandy ten-minute asides are historical garnish and carry no evidential weight. The mid-chapter premium-site promotion is advertising, never a source, and its prices are not claims.

  • Arrival reading: a timer starting at the front, rightly rejected.
  • Spotting reading: a timer starting at detection, the surviving claim.
  • Battlefield effect: being seen and killed quickly, frightening regardless of the exact figure.

  • The viral figure is admitted, then stress-tested with arithmetic.
  • The literal reading falls, and the conditional reading survives.
  • Drone dominance is corroborated by contemporaneous front reporting of drones falling almost daily, per the fortress-belt dispatch.

What survives is not a lifespan but a condition: once spotted, a Russian soldier's minutes are few, in line with the same drone-pressure record.
