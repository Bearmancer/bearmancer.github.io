3. Narrative

The relayed figure gives a recruit twenty to thirty minutes after reaching the battlefield, attributed to the CIA director through a paywalled outlet unreachable here. The relay is corrected from the viral "thirty minutes flat" version, and the outline is partially correct: consistent with open-source drone lethality, but resting on one relayed remark, not a measured study.

"It begins the moment they move forward, are spotted by a reconnaissance drone, and then become a target for a strike drone."

The reframing is the chapter's load-bearing move, and the arithmetic behind it is confirmed as internally valid illustration: forty-eight front updates a day and twenty-one men across a thousand-kilometer line are reductio math, not counts. The countdown-start correction itself is confirmed as the coherent reading.

The World War I fifteen-minute and Normandy ten-minute asides are historical garnish, unverified here and carrying no evidential weight. The mid-chapter premium-site promotion is labeled as advertising wherever it interrupts, never a source, and its prices are not claims.

  • Arrival reading: a timer starting at the front, rightly rejected.
  • Spotting reading: a timer starting at detection, the surviving claim.
  • Battlefield effect: being seen and killed quickly, frightening regardless of the exact figure.

4. Machinery

The chapter works by steelmanning a weak number into a strong mechanism.

  • Viral figure admitted, then stress-tested with arithmetic.
  • Literal reading demolished, conditional reading salvaged.
  • Drone dominance corroborated by contemporaneous front reporting of drones falling almost daily, per the fortress-belt dispatch.

What survives is not a lifespan but a condition: once spotted, a Russian soldier's minutes are few. That condition is partially correct against the same 1 drone-pressure record.
