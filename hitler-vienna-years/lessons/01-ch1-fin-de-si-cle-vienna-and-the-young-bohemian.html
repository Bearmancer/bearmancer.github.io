Fin-de-Siècle Vienna and the Young Bohemian

Hitler spent five years in imperial Vienna living on Stumpergasse while being rejected by the Academy of Fine Arts.12

Metropolitan Setting and Demographics

Hitler spent five years from 1908 to 1913 living in Vienna, the imperial capital of the Austro-Hungarian Empire.1

Hitler described his five years in Vienna from 1908 to 1913 in Mein Kampf as his most difficult and thorough school.1

Vienna was a major European metropolis of approximately two million inhabitants, making it Europe's fourth most populous city before World War I.3

Vienna's population doubled between 1880 and 1910, with Czech immigrants becoming the largest minority group, representing one-fifth of residents by 1910.4

More than 175,000 Jews lived in Vienna in 1910, representing 8.7 percent of the metropolitan population.4

Bohemian Life and Artistic Rejection

Hitler and August Kubizek rented a room together in Maria Zakrey's apartment on Stumpergasse in Vienna starting in late February 1908.5

Hitler spent nights reading Germanic mythology, heroic sagas, and architectural histories while living in Vienna.6

Hitler rejected Viennese modernism and abstract painting, preferring traditional romantic artists such as Arnold Böcklin and Eduard Grützner.7

Hitler applied to the Academy of Fine Arts in Vienna for a second time in September 1908 and was rejected without being invited to test.2
