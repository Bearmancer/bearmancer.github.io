The Anti-Semitism Myth and the Flight to Munich

Hitler maintained business ties with Jewish acquaintances in Vienna before using an inheritance payout to relocate to Munich.123

Deconstructing the Vienna Anti-Semitism Myth

Historian Brigitte Hamann demonstrated that Hitler's claim of an early anti-Semitic conversion in Vienna was a self-invented autobiographical myth.4

Hitler maintained cordial daily interactions and commercial partnerships with Jewish residents in the Meldemannstrasse men's home.1

Relocation to Munich and Draft Exemption

Hitler collected an inheritance payout of nearly 820 crowns from the district court in Linz on May 16, 1913.2

Hitler moved to Munich on May 25, 1913, renting a fourth-floor room from tailor Joseph Popp on Schleissheimerstrasse with Rudolf Häusler.3

A Salzburg military medical commission declared Hitler physically unfit for combat or auxiliary military service on February 5, 1914.5
