Political Influences, Destitution, and the Men's Home

In Vienna, Hitler encountered political currents that would later shape his worldview. He admired Georg Ritter von Schönerer and the pan-German movement he led. He also observed the political machine of Vienna's mayor, Karl Lueger, who modernized the city through municipalization of gas, electricity, and transit while expanding social healthcare benefits.

By late autumn 1909, Hitler had become completely destitute and sought shelter at the Meidling homeless shelter. On February 9, 1910, he and Reinhold Hanisch moved into the modern municipal men's home on Meldemannstrasse in the Brigittenau district. There, Hitler gradually established a measure of independence by selling his watercolor paintings directly to picture-frame dealers and art dealers, including Jakob Altenberg and Samuel Morgenstern.

Life in the men's home was not without conflict. On August 5, 1910, Hitler filed a formal complaint at the Brigittenau police station accusing Reinhold Hanisch of withholding revenue from his paintings. Despite the instability of this period, the Meldemannstrasse years gave Hitler a degree of autonomy and exposed him to the diverse, working-class milieu of Vienna.
