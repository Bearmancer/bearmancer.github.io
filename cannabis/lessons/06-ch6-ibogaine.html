Ibogaine

Ibogaine is being studied for opioid addiction, but a small monitored study found serious, reversible effects on heart rhythm and balance.123

The study

In an open-label observational study, 14 patients with opioid use disorder received a single 10 mg/kg dose of ibogaine hydrochloride and were monitored for at least 24 hours.1

Heart and balance effects

Ibogaine lengthened the heart's QTc interval by an average of 95 milliseconds, and half of the 14 patients briefly exceeded a QTc of 500 milliseconds; no torsades de pointes occurred.2

The study concluded ibogaine can cause clinically relevant but reversible QTc prolongation, slow heart rate, and severe loss of balance in people with opioid use disorder.3
