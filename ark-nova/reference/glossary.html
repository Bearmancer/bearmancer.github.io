Shared word list for the course. Lessons use these names for these things, spelled the way the rulebook spells them.

The Two Tracks

Appeal — The measure of how attractive your zoo is. Playing animals and building pavilions raise it, and each break it sets how much money you collect.

Conservation Points — The measure of your conservation work, advanced by supporting projects and by donations. Reaching 2, 5, 8, and 10 on the track brings one-time effects: an action upgrade or an extra worker at 2, money or a bonus token at 5 and 8, and a final scoring card discard at 10.

Reputation — The measure of your zoo's standing, tracked on the reputation track beside the display. It decides which display cards sit within your reputation range, and some sponsor cards need a minimum reputation before you may play them. Raising it to 10 or more needs the upgraded Cards action; the slice shows the space-10 reminder only as a folder icon, so its exact printed form stays flagged as ambiguous. A counter already on space 15 turns each further step of reputation into 1 appeal instead.

Break Track — The countdown on the game board toward the next break. Actions and card effects advance the break token, and reaching the last space calls a break for everyone after the current turn ends. The player who triggers it takes 1 X-token, and extra steps past the end are ignored.

The Action Cards

Cards Action — The action that gains new zoo cards from the deck or the display. Every use first advances the break token 2 spaces, with no money paid for those steps.

Build Action — The action that places buildings on your zoo map: standard enclosures, special enclosures, kiosks, and pavilions. The slice's strength figures did not survive extraction, so this entry claims no strength-to-size numbers.

Animals Action — The action that plays animal cards from your hand into empty enclosures, after checking each card's conditions and paying its cost.

Association Action — The action that sends association workers to the association board for reputation, partner zoos, partner universities, conservation project work, and, once upgraded, donations.

Sponsors Action — The action that plays sponsor cards into your zoo, or, instead of a card, advances the break token to take money.

X-Token Action — The fallback move used when you cannot or will not perform any of the five actions. Pick any action card, move it to slot 1, shift the rest right, and take exactly 1 X-token. Slot strength does nothing here, X-tokens cannot boost it, upgrades do not matter, and the move is blocked once you hold 5 X-tokens.

Upgraded Action (Side II) — An action card flipped to its improved side and usable in the stronger version for the rest of the game. Upgrades come from the conservation track, the reputation track, a second partner zoo, or a second partner university, so not every card can be upgraded in one game. The printed upgrade icon did not survive extraction, so the icon itself stays flagged as ambiguous.

Card Types

Zoo Cards — The three playable card types in the game: animal cards, sponsor cards, and conservation project cards.

Animal Cards — Cards played with the Animals action into an empty enclosure. Each one gives appeal and often adds icons, reputation, conservation points, or an ability.

Sponsor Cards — Cards played with the Sponsors action for specialists, unique buildings, or other effects. Many give ongoing effects or break income, and some pay appeal or conservation points at the end of the game.

Conservation Project Cards — Cards supported with the Association action to collect conservation points. Most ask for a count of icons in your zoo, while release projects ask you to discard a matching animal from your zoo instead.

Base Conservation Projects — Project cards available from the start of the game. They sit below the association board, are never moved, and are never replaced.

Final Scoring Cards — Held cards that add extra conservation points at the end of the game. Every player discards one of them, either when someone first reaches 10 conservation points or before final scoring if nobody does. The full scoring table stays out of this course on purpose.

Animal Categories — The seven icons grouping animals: bird, herbivore, predator, primate, reptile, bear, and petting zoo animal. Bear and petting zoo animal print black on white; bears mostly pair with herbivore or predator, and the petting zoo animal category was invented for the game rather than taken from biology.

The Board and Pieces

Zoo Map — Your personal board. It holds your buildings, partner zoos, universities, workers, money, X-tokens, and the numbered slots for your action cards.

Display — The six face-up cards in numbered folders beside the reputation track. Cards taken from it wait until your whole turn ends before higher-folder cards slide down and fresh deck cards fill the empty folders.

Association Board — The shared board carrying worker tasks, donation spaces, and the conservation projects above and below it.

Association Worker — The token spent from the notepad of your zoo map onto association board tasks. A first visit costs one worker, a repeat visit costs two, and a third repeat is blocked until the break returns everyone.

Partner Zoo — A tile cutting 3 money per continent icon off every animal card from its continent. It also counts as one icon of that continent and can satisfy card conditions. A zoo holds at most four partner zoos, all from different continents, with the third and fourth needing the upgraded Association action.

University — A tile granting reputation, research icons, and sometimes bonuses. Universities are the only way to raise the hand limit to 5 cards, and no university can be taken twice.

Kiosk — A size-1 building paying break income for each adjacent unique building, special enclosure, occupied standard enclosure, and pavilion. Kiosks must sit at least 3 spaces from any other kiosk, and an empty standard enclosure beside one pays nothing.

Pavilion — A size-1 building adding 1 appeal the moment it is built and counting as an adjacent building for kiosk income.

Money — The currency paid for buildings and animals and collected each break from appeal, kiosks, sponsor cards, and activated map bonuses.

Bonus Tiles — One-time bonus tokens beside spaces of the conservation track, taken when the track is reached or passed. The components list calls them bonus tiles while the track text calls them bonus tokens, so the name stays flagged as ambiguous.

Placement Bonus — A bonus printed on map spaces, taken at once when a building covers it, in time to fund further building in the same action.

Player Tokens — Markers tracking special-enclosure room and supported conservation projects. Seven start on the left edge of the zoo map and move onto project conditions, firing each space's bonus as they leave.

Enclosures

Standard Enclosure — A sized 1 to 5 tile housing exactly one animal. It is built empty-side up with a yellow edge showing its size and flipped to the occupied side when filled. Some animals need the tile beside rock or water spaces.

Special Enclosures — The petting zoo, the reptile house, and the large bird aviary, with at most one of each per zoo. Both sides of each tile are the same, so they never flip; used room is marked with player tokens instead.

Petting Zoo — The size-3 special enclosure and the only home for petting zoo animals, available to the basic Build action.

Reptile House — The size-5 special enclosure housing reptiles, needing the upgraded Build action. Building it lets you move matching animals out of standard enclosures in that same moment, and only then.

Large Bird Aviary — The size-5 special enclosure housing some birds, under the same building and moving rules as the reptile house.

Covered — A map space with a building on it. Covering a space triggers its placement bonus, and only empty spaces may be built on.
