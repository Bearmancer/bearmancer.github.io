Ark Nova: The Short Version
Aa

The Whole Game on One Page

You plan and build a modern, scientifically managed zoo. You place animals in enclosures that fit their way of life. You back conservation projects that protect species. The rulebook introduction states that premise: see the Ark Nova rulebook (Capstone Games, English).

Two tracks measure your zoo. Gathering diverse animals raises appeal and earns higher revenues. Supporting conservation projects advances conservation and brings bonuses.

  • Appeal climbs one way and pays money during each break.
  • Conservation climbs the other way and unlocks project rewards.
  • The two tracks run in opposite directions.

When your two counters meet, the game ends. More exactly, the end triggers when they land in the same scoring area or pass one another; the end-of-game section of the rulebook states the rule.

Scoring

The winner has the best combination of appeal and conservation. The rulebook's original formula reads "Your final score is simply how many spaces your appeal marker sits beyond your conservation marker on the appeal track." That formula is contested against the designer's 2023 scoring update, as quoted in the BGG thread on the 2023 rules updates:

"In the future, all conservation point spaces will feature a small number so that to determine your score, you only have to add the number of the space with your conservation point marker on it and your appeal to have your total score. No more calculating the distance between your 2 markers. Also the numbers are calculated in a way that having your 2 markers reach other will score you exactly 100 points. If your markers cross, your score will be above 100, if they dont, it will be below. But nobody should finish with a negative score anymore."

Under that update the old crossed-counters-mean-a-positive-score reading no longer holds as printed, while the meeting rule itself stands. Chapter 6 carries the full updated table. The slice does not give the counters' starting spaces, so no starting number appears here.

The Two Tracks as a Clock

Every appealing animal ticks one hand forward. Every conservation project ticks the other hand toward it.

  • Appeal income funds more building.
  • Conservation progress funds more projects.
  • The meeting point arrives faster when both move.

At the end, add the number printed on your conservation space to your appeal for the total score; meeting at 100 and crossing above it replaces the old crossed-counters-mean-positive reading.
