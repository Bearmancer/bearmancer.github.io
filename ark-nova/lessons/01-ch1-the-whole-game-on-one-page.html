1. Summary

You are building a modern zoo. Two counters move toward each other, and when they meet the game ends.

3. Narrative

You plan and build a modern, scientifically managed zoo. You place animals in enclosures that fit their way of life. You back conservation projects that protect species. That premise is confirmed in the rulebook introduction, beside the Ark Nova rulebook (Capstone Games, English).

Two tracks measure your zoo. Gathering diverse animals raises appeal and earns higher revenues. Supporting conservation projects advances conservation and brings bonuses.

  • Appeal climbs one way and pays money during each break.
  • Conservation climbs the other way and unlocks project rewards.
  • The two tracks run in opposite directions.

When your two counters meet, the game ends. More exactly, the end triggers when they land in the same scoring area or pass one another. That meeting rule is confirmed in the end-of-game section.

The winner has the best combination of appeal and conservation. The rulebook's original formula — "Your final score is simply how many spaces your appeal marker sits beyond your conservation marker on the appeal track." — is contested against the designer's 2023 scoring update, quoted in the BGG thread on the 2023 rules updates:

"In the future, all conservation point spaces will feature a small number so that to determine your score, you only have to add the number of the space with your conservation point marker on it and your appeal to have your total score. No more calculating the distance between your 2 markers. Also the numbers are calculated in a way that having your 2 markers reach other will score you exactly 100 points. If your markers cross, your score will be above 100, if they dont, it will be below. But nobody should finish with a negative score anymore."

Under that update the old crossed-counters-mean-a-positive-score reading no longer holds as printed; the meeting rule itself stays confirmed, and chapter 6 carries the full updated table. Exact starting spaces for the counters are unverified in this slice, so no starting number is given.

4. Machinery

Think of the two tracks as a clock. Every appealing animal ticks one hand forward. Every conservation project ticks the other hand toward it.

  • Appeal income funds more building.
  • Conservation progress funds more projects.
  • The meeting point arrives faster when both move.

At the end, add the number printed on your conservation space to your appeal for the total score; meeting at 100 and crossing above it replaces the old crossed-counters-mean-positive reading. Chapter 6 carries the full updated table.
