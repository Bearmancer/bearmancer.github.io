1. Summary

You are building a modern zoo. Two counters move toward each other, and when they meet the game ends.

3. Narrative

You plan and build a modern, scientifically managed zoo. You place animals in enclosures that fit their way of life. You back conservation projects that protect species. That premise is confirmed in the rulebook introduction, beside the Ark Nova rulebook (Capstone Games, English).

Two tracks measure your zoo. Gathering diverse animals raises appeal and earns higher revenues. Supporting conservation projects advances conservation and brings bonuses.

  • Appeal climbs one way and pays money during each break.
  • Conservation climbs the other way and unlocks project rewards.
  • The two tracks run in opposite directions.

When your two counters meet, the game ends. More exactly, the end triggers when they land in the same scoring area or pass one another. That meeting rule is confirmed in the end-of-game section.

The winner has the best combination of appeal and conservation. The rulebook notes your counters must cross for a positive score. Exact starting spaces for the counters are unverified in this slice, so no starting number is given.

4. Machinery

Think of the two tracks as a clock. Every appealing animal ticks one hand forward. Every conservation project ticks the other hand toward it.

  • Appeal income funds more building.
  • Conservation progress funds more projects.
  • The meeting point arrives faster when both move.

At the end, your conservation position sets a target number and your appeal is measured against it. Crossed counters mean a positive score.
