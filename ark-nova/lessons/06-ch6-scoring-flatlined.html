Scoring, Flatlined

Chapter 6 of 9

1. Summary

Two numbers decide everything. Appeal pulls in money each break, conservation points climb beside it, and the win goes to whoever finishes furthest past their target.

3. Narrative

There are two scores, and both matter. Appeal is your zoo's pull. Conservation points are your good work for nature. That two-track setup is confirmed in the rulebook's own wording, with background on the Ark Nova rulebook (Capstone Games, English).

"In order to have a positive score, your appeal counter and conservation counter have to cross one another!"

Appeal also pays you during the game. Each break, you collect money based on your spot on the appeal track. Conservation helps during the game too, with one-time effects at certain spaces. Neither track runs forever: the official FAQ V2 EN caps them — "No, you cannot gain more than 113 Appeal or 41 conversation points." — and that ceiling is confirmed per the same 2 ruling.

The game ends when the two counters meet. Once your conservation counter and appeal counter sit in the same scoring area, or pass one another, the end is triggered. Each scoring area is one conservation space plus its neighboring appeal spaces. If that happens at the end of your turn, every other player takes one more turn. If it happens during a break, everyone takes one more turn.

Before the final tally, a few cards still pay out. Your remaining final scoring card can add one to four conservation points. Cards with an end-of-game icon add their appeal or conservation points too. Where losing appeal and gaining conservation points collide, the order is confirmed by the FAQ — "You lose the appeal before you gain the conservation points." — per the same 2 ruling.

One discard rule shapes those cards. The first time anyone reaches ten conservation points, every player discards one final scoring card. If nobody reaches ten before the game ends, everyone discards one before final scoring begins. No single final scoring card ever pays more than four conservation points.

  • SPONSORED ZOO (card 800): count the sponsor cards in your zoo — 3, 6, 8, or 10 of them earns 1, 2, 3, or 4 conservation points respectively.
  • BIODIVERSE ZOO (card 600): gain 1 conservation point for each animal category icon of which you have more than the player to your right, up to 4. In solo play, do not use this card — draw another.

The rulebook's printed formula turns the two counters into victory points:

"Begin by looking at the scoring area where your conservation counter is located. Find the lowest appeal value in that scoring area. This is your Target Number. Compare your Target Number to the value of your appeal counter. Subtract your Target Number from your appeal value and the result is your Victory Points."

That printed formula is contested by the designer's 2023 scoring update, quoted in the BGG thread on the 2023 rules updates:

"In the future, all conservation point spaces will feature a small number so that to determine your score, you only have to add the number of the space with your conservation point marker on it and your appeal to have your total score. No more calculating the distance between your 2 markers. Also the numbers are calculated in a way that having your 2 markers reach other will score you exactly 100 points. If your markers cross, your score will be above 100, if they dont, it will be below. But nobody should finish with a negative score anymore."

The update itself is confirmed as the designer's current rule; the old crossed-counters arithmetic survives only in outdated secondary guides — "Your final score is simply how many spaces your appeal marker sits beyond your conservation marker on the appeal track." and "Second, negative scores are normal. It doesn't mean you played poorly." — which no longer hold under the update. Chapter 1 flags this same split.

Under the printed scoring, the worked example runs exactly as the rulebook shows. Blue holds 16 conservation points against a target number of 76 but only 72 appeal, finishing 4 short at −4 victory points. Red holds 18 conservation points against a target of 70 with 79 appeal, finishing 9 beyond at +9. The trigger example shows Red at 64 appeal and 20 conservation points, both counters inside one scoring area, ending the game.

  • Push appeal up, since it pays each break and forms your final score.
  • Push conservation up, since it sets an easier target and must cross appeal.
  • Keep cards that pay at the end, since they add points after the last turn.

The winner is whoever finishes furthest past their target. You look at the scoring area holding your conservation counter, take the lowest appeal value there as your target number, and subtract it from your appeal. The highest positive total wins. Tied players break the tie by counting supported conservation projects.

4. Machinery

Picture two markers racing toward each other on two tracks. Your appeal marker climbs one side. Your conservation marker climbs the other. The finish line is the point where they meet.

  • Scoring areas link the tracks: each conservation space pairs with neighboring appeal spaces.
  • Your target is the lowest appeal value inside your conservation scoring area.
  • Victory points equal your appeal minus that target, so crossing early with high appeal wins.
  • Under the 2023 update, add the number printed on your conservation space to your appeal instead, with 100 as the meeting point.

That is why both tracks pull double duty. Appeal feeds your wallet at every break and your final total. Conservation unlocks effects along the way and lowers the bar you must clear.
