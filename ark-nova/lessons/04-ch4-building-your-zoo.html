Building Your Zoo

Chapter 4 of 9

1. Summary

The Build action raises empty enclosures, kiosks, and pavilions, and the Animals action fills those homes with appeal-earning residents.

3. Narrative

Your zoo grows in two moves. First you place empty homes with the Build action. Then you fill them with the Animals action. That build-then-fill loop is confirmed as the spine of these pages in the Ark Nova rulebook (Capstone Games, English).

Build places exactly one building at base strength. Every space costs 2 money, so cost equals size times two. You pay first, then you place.

At base strength you can choose a kiosk or a pavilion at size 1, a standard enclosure from size 1 to 5, or the Petting Zoo special enclosure at size 3. One zoo holds only one Petting Zoo. Other buildings can repeat.

Kiosks pay you income during every break. Each pavilion adds 1 appeal at once, the moment you build it. Standard enclosures go down empty-side up, with a yellow edge showing their size, ready for a later animal.

  • New buildings must touch an existing one along at least one edge. Your first building must sit on the map border. The official FAQ V2 EN defines that edge rule: "A border space is a space on your zoo plan that does not have another space on at least 1 of its 6 sides."
  • Build only on empty spaces. Never overlap, never leave the map, never cover water or rock.
  • Spaces with a restricted mark need an upgraded Build action first.
  • Kiosks must sit at least 3 spaces from any other kiosk, with 2 spaces between them.
  • Covering a placement bonus mark pays that bonus at once.

Upgraded Build opens the Large Bird Aviary and the Reptile House, both size 5, one of each per zoo. It also lets you raise several different buildings in a single action and build on restricted spaces. The exact strength-to-size numbers are unverified in this slice because the extraction dropped the strength glyphs, so this lesson claims no numbers for them.

Animals come next. Action strength sets how many Animal cards you may play from your hand. Each animal runs the same five steps. Check every condition on the left of the card. Pay the full price in the upper-left corner. Fill a free enclosure that meets the size in the hexagon. Lay the card beside your map. Then take what it gives.

The price carries one discount worth knowing. A partner zoo on the animal's home continent cuts 3 money per continent icon. You must still hold the full price before discounts from other cards arrive, since those pay out later.

The card layout splits cleanly into demands and rewards. The top-left hexagon sets the smallest standard enclosure the animal accepts, with small rock and water marks adding adjacency demands. The left edge lists play conditions and the lower price. The top-right names the category and home continent. The bottom-right shows the appeal gain, sometimes with conservation points or reputation. Most animals add one ability, played at once unless marked after finishing. Fine icon meanings live on the icon reference page, which is confirmed as the right next stop rather than guessing here.

  • One animal per empty standard enclosure, then flip the tile to its occupied side. The flip follows the printings 1.0/1.1 erratum in the same 2 FAQ source: "You must flip the smallest possible occupied enclosure that meets all of the enclosure requirements of the animal (including water and rock spaces). In the rare case there is no such enclosure, flip the smallest occupied enclosure that meets at least the animal's enclosure size requirement. If there is no occupied enclosure at all that meets the animal's enclosure size requirement, do not flip any enclosure."
  • Petting Zoo animals fit only the Petting Zoo, never a standard enclosure.
  • Reptiles and some birds show two requirements, so you pick standard or special housing.
  • Water and rock demands follow the animal into the special enclosure too.
  • After-finishing abilities wait until the whole Animals action ends. Simultaneous rewards resolve in the order you choose.

An upgraded Animals action reaches beyond your hand. Cards on the display inside your reputation range become playable, plus the number shown on the folder you take from. Strength 5 or better also raises your reputation by 1 first, widening range before you pick. The display refills only after your turn ends.

Special enclosures bend the one-animal-per-tile rule. Your zoo holds at most one Petting Zoo, one Reptile House, and one Large Bird Aviary. Special tiles never flip. You mark used room with player tokens instead, per the 1 record.

New specials can absorb old residents. Building the Reptile House or Aviary lets you move matching animals out of standard enclosures that same moment, and only then. Each moved animal frees its old tile back to empty. Each move is optional, decided animal by animal. The same 2 FAQ source confirms the timing: "You may immediately move all corresponding animals in your zoo."

The slice's caiman example stays partially correct as printed here. A Broad-snouted Caiman fits a 5-space standard enclosure beside water, or the Reptile House for 2 tokens. The enclosure logic reads clean. The surrounding glyphs did not survive extraction, so treat the token counts as the slice's words, not as checked art.

4. Machinery

Empty homes first, residents second. That order shapes every turn.

  • Money timing matters. You pay before you place, and bonus money from a covered mark arrives in time to fund the next building in the same action.
  • Adjacency shapes the map. Edge contact and border starts push growth outward in one connected zoo.
  • Enclosure size is a gate, not a cost. Bigger tiles accept smaller animals, but one tile still holds one animal outside specials.
  • Specials consolidate. Moving reptiles and birds into one house reopens standard tiles for big animals that fit nowhere else.
  • Abilities split by timing. Immediate rewards compound inside the action, while after-finishing rewards land only when the action closes.

When choosing, build the home one turn before the animal arrives. An empty tile is a promise. A filled tile is appeal.
