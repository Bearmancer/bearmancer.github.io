1. Summary

Marine Worlds adds sea life to your zoo and leaves the finish line alone. New animals, new tanks, and new twists on familiar cards, all safe to skip until you know the base game.

3. Narrative

This note follows the expansion's account rather than quoting it. The rulebook PDF has no text layer, so every detail here is reconstructed from a visual read of the page images. The headline additions are confirmed against the Marine Worlds page on BoardGameGeek and the publisher's German expansion rules, while fine mechanical details stay unverified.

Marine Worlds adds sea animals. They carry an octopus icon. Some animals hold two categories at once, and the expansion's example is a shark that counts as both sea animal and predator.

  • Two Aquarium enclosures: a 2-space Small aquarium and a 5-space Large one. Both sizes are confirmed in the visual read — "This is the large aquarium. It covers 5 spaces." and "This is the small aquarium. It covers 2 spaces." — with at most one of each: "You can build both 1 large and 1 small aquarium in your zoo. Like all other special enclosures, you can build a maximum of 1 each, so you are not allowed to build 2 of the same aquarium type." Both must go "next to at least 1 water space," and both build on Side I of Build with no upgrade: "You do not have to upgrade the Build Action card to be able to build them." The community review agrees they work "even with your basic starting Build card" and "require a space next to water," per the dudetakeyourturn Marine Worlds review, seconded by the Board Game Arena game help.
  • Reef Dwellers: about half the sea animals. Playing one triggers every Reef effect you own, including its own, in an order you choose. That trigger is confirmed in the visual read:
"Whenever you play a Reef Dweller card, trigger the Reef Dweller effects of all Reef Dwellers in your zoo (including the Reef Dweller just played). That way, you can get the Reef Dweller effect of an animal multiple times, not just once."
  • The Wave icon: quiet when played, busy during replenish. "This icon has no effect when playing the card." Instead, each Wave churns the display on replenish, which is confirmed:
"whenever you replenish the display and add a card with the wave icon on it, discard the bottom card (folder 1) of the display and replenish again. In the rare case that you reveal 2 (or more) cards with a wave icon at the same time, discard 1 card for each wave icon (so the bottom 2 or more)."

The setup exception is likewise confirmed:

"Ignore the wave icon during setup."
  • A new WAZA university set: one generic university plus six specific ones. It grants an animal icon of your choice, finds a matching card, and adds a research icon. The two take conditions and the per-player cap are confirmed:
"When using an Association action to gain a partner university, you may take a new university only if both of the following are true: 1. The generic university (the one with the rainbow animal icon) is still on the Association board. 2. You do not have any of the new universities yet."
"each player can have a maximum of 1 new university."

The community lane agrees on the choice-and-search effect — "When you take that university, you choose which animal tag you want to use. Not only does the university count as that animal tag, but you then get to search through the deck for a card with that tag." — per the same 6 review and the BGG thread on the animal-icon university.

Calling it a new game would be wrong. The new cards shuffle into the existing decks, per the Capstone Games page for Ark Nova: Marine Worlds. Eighteen base-game cards are directly replaced, which is confirmed by the printed Attention box:

"Attention! The following cards replace cards from the base game: 001, 003, 005, 008, 009, 010, 011, 101, 102, 131, 207, 208, 225, 226, 227, 250, 261, 262. Please make sure to remove the respective older components."

Older pieces get touch-ups rather than rewrites.

  • The "different animal types" icon now counts sea animals.
  • A replacement Association board reshapes the university area.
  • The base Action cards are reprinted. They play the same and now allow aquariums, with shorter text and source icons.
  • Each action also gains numbered alternates. 0 is the standard card, and 1 through 4 are variants with source icons above the name band.
  • New grey-backed Bonus tiles arrive later than the base ones.

Before play, each player drafts two alternate Action cards of different types into their set. That is the whole draft: two swaps, two different actions, then the game plays as usual.

Scoring stays put. The expansion does not change the final scoring formula or the end-game trigger. It only adds cards, including new Final Scoring and Conservation cards. One extra prize exists: passing 15 reputation earns a bonus tile, or 1 appeal if the tile is gone.

Marine Worlds is for players who already know the base game. For a first play, ignore it entirely.

4. Machinery

Under the hood, the expansion widens the card pool without touching the engine. That is the whole trick, per the Feuerland Spiele page for Arche Nova Wasserwelten.

  • More cards in the same decks means slower searches and longer cycles. The Wave icon answers that drift by churning the display on replenish — confirmed in the 4 publisher rules — and the display-reading habits from chapter 8 pair naturally with it, per the broom9 display analysis and the boardgamestrategy.blog early-rounds analysis.
  • Reef Dwellers reward density. Each sea animal you add makes the next one louder, since every play re-triggers the whole set.
  • Aquariums gate that snowball behind space and water. Two tanks at most, Side I of Build, so sea plans stay compact.
  • Alternate Action cards swap tools, not goals. Two different swaps per player keeps variety up and the finish line fixed.
