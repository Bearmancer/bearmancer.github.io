Cards: The Real Bottleneck

Everything you build starts as a zoo card, and there are exactly three kinds; the Ark Nova rulebook (Capstone Games, English) describes the same card-driven game.

  • Animal cards add animals to your zoo. Each one needs an empty enclosure and gives your zoo appeal.
  • Conservation Project cards support projects and collect conservation points. Many focus on certain species or continents, and some reintroduce animals into the wild. Base projects of this type are available from the start.
  • Sponsor cards hire specialists, build unique buildings, or produce other one-off effects.

The Break Track

The clock for all of this is the Break track. The Break token moves toward the end of the track as actions advance it.

Reaching the last space calls a break for everyone after the current turn ends. The player who triggers it takes 1 X-token, and extra steps past the end are ignored. After the break, the token goes back to its starting space.

The Display and Reputation

Next to the board sits the display: 6 face-up cards in numbered folders, beside the Reputation track. Your counter on that track measures your zoo's reputation.

Raising reputation means moving your counter 1 step. Landing on a bonus space gives that bonus immediately, only once. A counter already on space 15 converts further reputation into 1 appeal per step. The slice shows a folder icon rather than words at space 10, so no reminder text appears for it here.

Reputation decides which display cards you can simply take. Everything on your counter's folder plus all lower-numbered folders counts as within range. Higher reputation means more cards in reach.

The Cards Action

The basic Cards action always starts the same way. Advance the Break token 2 spaces, with no money paid for those steps.

  • Draw from the deck: check the action strength against the table, draw up to 3 cards, then possibly discard one card from your hand.
  • Snap: at strength 5 or higher, skip drawing and take exactly 1 card from the display instead. Any card is legal, even outside your reputation range.

An upgraded Cards action widens both options. Drawing may mix display cards within reputation range with deck draws, in any order. Snapping drops to strength 3, not the basic strength 5. Only the upgraded action can carry reputation to 10 and beyond.

Taken display cards wait until your whole turn ends before refills happen. Higher-folder cards slide down in order, and fresh deck cards fill the empty top folders. The discard pile beside the display stays face-down and off-limits to browsing.

So the bottleneck is simple. Your plans do not matter until your hand and the display offer the right card at the right strength.

The Deck as Throttle, the Display as Window

  • Blind draws give volume, up to 3 cards, but no choice of what arrives.
  • Reputation-range picks give choice, but only from folders your counter has unlocked.
  • Snapping gives precision, exactly 1 card regardless of range, but ends the draw entirely.

The refill rule keeps every pick visible to the table. Sliding cards down in order, then topping up from the deck, means everyone watches the window change at turn end.

Reputation is the slow gate. Early on, most of the display sits above your counter and only snapping can reach it. Upgrading the Cards action lifts both the range rule and the ceiling past 9.

The Break token ties card income to the shared clock. Every Cards action pushes the break 2 steps closer, and the player who lands the last step banks an X-token.
