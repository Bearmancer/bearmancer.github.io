1. Summary

The video tours Saudi troops, budgets, and imported hardware, then argues the force underperforms its wealth and neighbors, a claim this chapter checks line by line.

3. Narrative

The chapter piles up numbers to argue that money does not equal muscle.

"The nation boasts a total active duty troop count of 260,000."

The headcount is confirmed as a fair round: derived tables show about 257,000 active troops plus reserves.

The spending line is partially correct: the 2024 actual was near 80 billion dollars at about seven percent of output, ranked seventh.

The standing army slice is confirmed at about seventy-five thousand soldiers.

"Over half belong to the Saudi Arabian National Guard."

That share is wrong: the Guard holds roughly one hundred thousand to one hundred thirty thousand of a quarter-million-strong force, which is under a separate ministry but below half.

"Its military is split in half for no clear reason at all."

The motive claim is also wrong: parallel militaries are textbook coup-proofing, a deliberate regime-safety design per coup-proofing scholarship.

The Guard detail that survives is confirmed: its core duty is shielding the ruling family against coups.

  • Turkey comparison is partially correct: about four hundred eighty-one thousand active and three hundred eighty thousand reserve.
  • Iran comparison is partially correct: about five hundred seventy thousand active and three hundred fifty thousand reserve.
  • Egypt comparison is confirmed: about four hundred thirty-eight thousand active and four hundred seventy-nine thousand reserve on under five billion dollars.
  • Israel and Iraq sit in the same order of magnitude but clearly smaller, so that line is partially correct: Israel holds about one hundred sixty-nine thousand active troops and Iraq about one hundred ninety-three thousand.

The trainer verdict is partially correct: harsh foreign assessments are documented, from a 1991 coalition commander calling Saudi ground units the weakest with inept commanders.

But the word most is unverified: no survey of foreign trainers exists, so the quantifier stays an allegation.

4. Machinery

Two parallel ground structures divide the manpower: the regular army under the defense ministry and the Guard under its own ministry.

The split weakens combined warfighting but strengthens regime survival, which is why rulers keep it.

Hardware flows inward from abroad: tanks count near several hundred modernized Abrams, artillery and rocket fleets number in the hundreds per inventory tables, and the air fleet blends American strike jets with over one hundred fifty European fighters.

Air defenses rest on a dedicated branch plus new foreign surface-to-air buys such as the Korean missile deal.

The system logic is import, duplicate, and divide: buy top-shelf kit abroad, overlap suppliers for political cover, and keep parallel chains loyal to the throne.
