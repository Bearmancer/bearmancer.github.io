The Identity Tangle: Peoples of Pakistan and Its Neighbours

Pakistan has only four land neighbours, yet three of them have exchanged fire with it in recent years. A Places video tours each frontier with a single device: how likely the neighbour would be to lift a finger in Pakistan's defence.

"we'll rank each neighbor according to how likely it would be to lift a finger in Pakistan's defense."

The answer the video builds runs through peoples, not governments: Bengalis, Kashmiris, Pashtuns, and Baloch, each cut by a border that never settled. The video's closing argument is that geography built for a bridge became a fortress.

Two Pakistans, One Religion

In the Places video's telling, Pakistan began in 1947 as two wings of one state, over 1,500 kilometres apart.

"Pakistan emerged in two separate pieces, West Pakistan, today is Pakistan, and East Pakistan, today is Bangladesh, which lies more than 1,500 km away in the eastern half of Bengal."

The founding logic was the Muslim League's case that Muslims would be unsafe as a permanent minority in a Hindu-majority India — that "the Muslims of British India constituted a separate nation requiring a separate homeland."

Partition then sorted populations by faith with brutal speed. In West Pakistan, Hindus fell from almost 4 million (14.6 percent) in 1941 to just over half a million (1.58 percent) a decade later, and some 4.7 to 5.0 million Hindus and Sikhs migrated to India, per the census record.

Bengal itself was cut in two by the Radcliffe Line: Hindu-majority West Bengal went to India while Muslim-majority East Bengal went to Pakistan, per the partition record, with about 2.04 million Bengali Hindus moving west and 1.72 million Bengali Muslims moving east in the immediate aftermath.

Faith alone could not hold the two wings together, because language came first for the east. Urdu was imposed as the sole national language though native to a minority; Bengali protests culminated in police firing on demonstrators in February 1952, and Bengali was recognised as a second state language in 1956, per the language-movement record. When the Awami League won 167 of 313 seats in the 1970 election and was denied power, per the election record, the break became a matter of time.

Bengal, Bangladesh, West Bengal

"Bengali" names a language community, not a country. The 1947 line left Hindu-majority West Bengal inside India and Muslim-majority East Bengal inside Pakistan — and East Pakistan's Bengalis, more numerous than West Pakistanis, found themselves ruled from across the subcontinent.

The 1971 crackdown and its toll remain the bitterest numbers in the story. Bangladesh maintains 3 million dead, while independent researchers estimate 300,000 to 500,000, per the genocide scholarship. Hindus, roughly a seventh of the population, were singled out — disproportionately, possibly a majority of those killed — and some 10 million refugees fled to India.

"it had demonstrated that Pakistan's Islamic identity was insufficient to hold together Bengalis, Punjabis, Sindhis, Pashtuns, and Baloch when political and linguistic grievances were ignored."

East Pakistan became Bangladesh rather than joining India because the liberation war forged a separate nationalism, with the war at the centre of Bangladeshi identity. The video's account traces the political camps dividing ever since over secular Bengali nationalism, sovereignty, and the collaboration question — and notes Pakistan recognised Bangladesh in February and signed a tripartite repatriation agreement that April.

The Urdu-Speakers in the Middle

Between the two Bengals stood a third community: the Urdu-speaking Muslims — Biharis and other Muhajirs — who emigrated from India and settled in East Pakistan after 1947, per the stranded-Pakistanis record. Under the Urdu regime they were privileged; in 1971 many sided with West Pakistan.

That choice left them stranded in every sense. Known afterward as stranded Pakistanis, they lived in camps and remained effectively stateless until a 2008 High Court ruling granted citizenship to minors and those born later. Resentment of Urdu privilege had helped fuel Bengali nationalism; the stigma of 1971 left the Urdu-speakers paying for it.

Kashmir: Muslim People, Hindu Prince

At partition, Jammu and Kashmir paired a Muslim-majority population with a Hindu ruler, Maharaja Hari Singh, who held out for independence until October 1947, when tribal incursions from the Pakistani side forced him to trade accession for Indian military help, per the Hanover account. The accession, dated 26 October 1947, is read two ways: India blames Pakistan for directing the invasion, while Pakistan denies official ordering.

Four wars followed — 1947, 1965, 1971, 1999 — plus the insurgency burning in Indian-administered Kashmir since 1989, with the 2001 Parliament assault, the 2008 Mumbai killings, and the 2019 Pulwama bombing each blamed on Pakistan-based groups. The video names Gilgit-Baltistan as the Pakistani-held north, and counts today's shares at roughly 30 percent Pakistan, 15 percent China, the rest India.

The latest rupture came on 22 April 2025, when gunmen killed 26 tourists near Pahalgam; India blamed militants with Pakistani links and suspended the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty, closed Attari-Wagah, and voided SAARC visas, per the attack reporting. The video's account continues the story into a four-day exchange in May 2025 — Indian strikes on nine sites, Pakistani retaliation, 66 combined civilian dead — with the treaty still in abeyance into 2026. Small wonder the video's rating here dips below zero.

"It is zero. In fact, it's less than zero."

The Durand Blur

The Afghan frontier was drawn in 1893, when Afghan ruler Abdul Rahman Khan and British official Mortimer Durand delimited spheres of influence across the Hindu Kush — a line that divided Pashtun tribes without attempting a culturally coherent boundary, per the Durand study. Afghanistan has never accepted its legitimacy, per the Yale history, and was the only country to vote against Pakistan's UN admission in September 1947 over it, per the Brookings account.

Successive Afghan governments promoted Pashtunistan — an autonomous or independent Pashtun territory taking in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and northern Balochistan, per the Pashtunistan record. Uniting those areas would carve an enormous chunk from Pakistan's centre, "converting its shape into something like that of Croatia," which is why Pashtuns on both sides are described as chafing under Islamabad's domination, per the Elcano analysis.

Pakistan answered with "strategic depth": the Mujahideen conduit in the 1980s, then the Taliban in the 1990s, then shelter for its leadership after 2001 while formally allied with Washington. When Kabul fell in 2021, Imran Khan crowed that Afghans had "broken the chains of slavery" — then watched the Taliban refuse the border. The doctrine's yield gets one damning line in the video.

"Instead, it repeatedly gave Pakistan strategic blowback."

The blowback has a name: the Tehrik-i-Taliban, or Pakistani Taliban — separate from the Afghan movement but sharing ideology and operational links — which seeks to overthrow or transform the Pakistani state and carried out the 2014 Peshawar school massacre of 149 people, including 132 children. After the Taliban's 2021 return, TTP violence surged from Afghan soil, Pakistan says; Kabul calls it an internal Pakistani problem. The video's account runs the spiral forward: mass deportations of Afghans, airstrikes inside Afghanistan, an "open war" declaration in February 2026, and a cited UN tally of 499 civilian dead — all while the Taliban tore down border fencing and opened contacts with India. The video's rating for Kabul is a flat zero.

The Baloch Third Frontier

The Baloch are divided among Iran, Pakistan, and Afghanistan without a country of their own, per the West Point study — an ethno-linguistic group spread across the three states, per the GWU archive, with Iran's Sistan-Baluchistan left underdeveloped and impoverished. Both Balochistans host insurgencies that mirror each other: Iran accuses Jaish al-Adl of using Pakistani soil, Pakistan accuses separatists of using Iran's — charges the video's account follows into the January 2024 missile exchange, when each side struck across the border and then restored ties.

Iran's quarrel with Pakistan runs on two tracks in the video's account: sectarian and separatist. Under the Shah both states aligned with Washington; after the revolution, a Shia revolutionary Iran faced a Pakistan drifting toward a Sunni, Saudi-partnered order during the Afghan war, with each accused of financing the other's sectarian networks. The economic track is need without trust: Pakistan short of electricity and gas beside Iran's reserves, while the peace pipeline stalled on financing and sanctions pressure. The video rates the relationship 3 out of 10.

China's stake runs through the same province. The video's account traces the partnership from Pakistan's early recognition of the PRC through the 1963 frontier agreement to the 2015 corridor worth some $65 billion toward Gwadar — and the Baloch separatist attacks on Chinese workers that followed, with Beijing pressing for its own security presence. The video rates Beijing 7 out of 10: diplomacy, parts, and money, but no direct war against India.

Gwadar itself carries the strangest title deed on the coast. The port spent some two centuries as an Omani possession; Oman offered it to India in the 1950s, New Delhi declined, and Pakistan bought it in 1958 for three million pounds, per the ICSF account — with a fourteen-point agreement, a reported ten percent of oil profits for twenty-five years, and 2,400 square miles reclaimed that September, per the purchase history.

Oman, the seller, remains the maritime neighbour across the Arabian Sea: training cooperation, a large Pakistani expatriate community, and a mediator's ties to everyone, which the video's account says keeps it friendly but out of any war not its own. The video puts Omani support at 6 out of 10 — warships much lower.

Why Each Neighbour's Line Is Still Live

India's line runs through Kashmir and now the waters treaty held in abeyance. Bangladesh's runs through memory and money: Dhaka demands a formal apology for 1971 plus $4.52 billion in assets, per the TRT report, against Pakistan's 1974 expression of regret and Musharraf's 2002 written regret for the excesses — welcomed, but never the full apology. The video's account adds a 2025–26 thaw of talks, rice shipments, scholarships, and political continuity under Tarique Rahman — leverage against Delhi, not an alliance, rated 3 out of 10.

Afghanistan's line is the Durand Line plus the TTP sanctuaries. Iran stays wary but pragmatic, China the genuine partner, Oman mostly friendly. The through-line, in the video's closing argument: Pakistan was founded on the promise of security for its Muslims, yet created profound distrust on almost every frontier — bridge geography turned fortress by policy.
