Missed Opportunities

If Riyadh will not build an arsenal for its own army, why not build one to sell? A rich kingdom diversifying under Vision 2030 could plausibly build weapons for export even if it will not field them itself. The record gives a split answer.

The Fighter-Program Rebuff

The video says Saudi Arabia asked to join the next-generation fighter program and was rebuffed, with a European executive telling Riyadh to assemble existing jets first. The request and the rebuff are on record in the February 2025 remarks of Leonardo co-director general Lorenzo Mariani, who floated Eurofighter assembly as a stepping stone.

The scornful tone around that reply is the video's own editorializing, not the executive's wording.

Riyadh's Own Ranking of Exports

"We haven't even spent a second thinking about what would be the target. Export is on our agenda, but it's not our priority at the moment."

That 2019 answer comes verbatim from the Defense News interview with the Saudi manufacturing governor, given when the export-controls framing was put to him directly.

Together the two episodes explain the missed opening. Foreign partners gate the technology, and Riyadh itself ranked exports below domestic capacity — so the export complex stayed an agenda item rather than a program.

The Defense-Industrial Ladder

The defense-industrial opportunity works like a ladder: licensed maintenance at the bottom, co-production in the middle, and clean-sheet exportable designs at the top.

Saudi firms climbed the lower rungs through joint ventures, including the corvette combat-management system built with a Spanish shipbuilder and drone co-production accords carrying technology transfer.

The top rung stays blocked by export controls and partner caution. Programs with the tightest technology share their work only among trusted members, which is why the fighter-program door stayed shut.

Until partners open that door, the import mix persists: American systems dominate at roughly three-quarters of recent imports, with Spain and France next, per the SIPRI arms-transfers factsheet.
