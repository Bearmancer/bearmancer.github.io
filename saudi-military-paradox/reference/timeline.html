Timeline

As of chapter 6, the course's dated claims run from the founding of the domestic arms industry to the latest spending data. Each entry states its standing in plain words.

  • 2017. The Public Investment Fund announces the founding of Saudi Arabian Military Industries and puts domestic military procurement at only around two percent (fund announcement).
  • 2018. Anthony Cordesman writes in a CSIS assessment that Saudi security decisions have not been part of any coherent force improvement plan (assessment text).
  • 2019. The GAMI governor tells Defense News that exports are on the agenda but not the priority, answering the sanctions question directly (interview transcript).
  • 2023. Localization reaches 19.35 percent by the end of the year (trade reporting), and the Baykar drone deal includes technology transfer and joint production (deal notice).
  • 2024. GAMI reports localization of 24.89 percent (year-end report) across 311 licensed establishments (permit registry); Saudi Arabia signs the $3.2 billion KM-SAM order with South Korea (deal coverage); Germany resumes IRIS-T deliveries after the 2018 ban (resumption notice); and the United States holds 74 percent of Saudi arms imports for the 2020 to 2024 window (transfer data).
  • May 2025. The White House announces a $142 billion defense sales package with Saudi Arabia (fact sheet), not the rounded $150 billion.
  • September 2025. The course source video is published.
  • 2026. SIPRI's 2025 factsheet records $83.2 billion in Saudi spending, eighth place, at 6.5 percent of output (factsheet), after the 2024 ranking put the kingdom at $80.3 billion and seventh (prior release).
