Deep-Cut 19th-Century Symphonies: Berwald, Farrenc, Beach

2026-09-30

Three symphonies from outside the overplayed canon, each checked against two recordings for timing.

Berwald, Symphony No. 3 "Singulière"

Franz Berwald (1796-1868) — Symphony No. 3 in C major, "Sinfonie singulière" (1845) [2 flutes, 2 oboes, 2 clarinets, 2 bassoons, 4 horns, 2 trumpets, 3 trombones, timpani, strings; 27:47 Goodman – 29:14 Kamu]

  • Wikipedia: written in 1845, premiered on 10 January 1905 in Stockholm, 37 years after Berwald's death; three movements (Allegro fuocoso, Adagio - Scherzo - Adagio, Finale: Presto in C minor ending in C major).
  • MusicWeb International review: "remarkably prophetic moments in which Sibelius seems to smile through the Beethovenian athleticism"; finale brass "especially brazen".
  • Timing: Hyperion lists 27:47 (Goodman); Naxos 8.553052 tracks sum to 29:14 (Kamu: 11:47 + 9:02 + 8:25).

Start with: the opening Allegro fuocoso, which conductor Roy Goodman's Hyperion booklet note calls "so remarkable"; listen for the optimistic drive the note contrasts with the sombre Adagio that encloses the scherzo.

Recording: Roy Goodman / Swedish Radio Symphony Orchestra / Hyperion (recorded October 1995, Berwaldhallen, Stockholm).

Farrenc, Symphony No. 3

Louise Farrenc (1804-1875) — Symphony No. 3 in G minor, Op. 36 (1847) [2 flutes, 2 oboes, 2 clarinets, 2 bassoons, 2 horns, timpani, strings per IMSLP; 30:45 Goritzki – 34:16 König]

  • IMSLP: composed 1847, first performed 22 April 1849 in Paris; movements Adagio - Allegro, Adagio cantabile, Scherzo (Vivace), Finale (Allegro).
  • Wikipedia: the third symphony was performed at the Société des concerts du Conservatoire in 1849.
  • Timing: Naxos 8.573706 tracks sum to 34:16 (König: 12:02 + 8:05 + 7:38 + 6:31); Classic Produktion Osnabrück (Goritzki) sums to 30:45 (8:49 + 9:27 + 5:49 + 6:40); Erato (Equilbey) sums to 33:27.

Start with: movement I; the cited sources give no movement-level analysis, so no listening cue is claimed.

Recording: Christoph König / Solistes Européens, Luxembourg / Naxos 8.573706.

Beach, "Gaelic" Symphony

Amy Beach (1867-1944) — Symphony in E minor, Op. 32, "Gaelic" (1894) [forces unverified, dropped; 35:27 Krueger – 42:04 Schermerhorn]

  • Wikipedia: written in 1894, premiered in Boston on 30 October 1896, the first symphony composed and published by an American woman; it uses three Irish melodies, "The Lily of the West" in the second movement.
  • Wikipedia, Amy Beach: premiered by the Boston Symphony Orchestra in 1896.
  • Timing: Naxos 8.559139 tracks sum to 42:04 (Schermerhorn: 10:46 + 7:26 + 13:04 + 10:48); Bridge 9086 sums to 35:27 (7:47 + 8:25 + 12:44 + 6:31). The large gap reflects tempo and possible cuts; the Bridge release was not checked for cuts.

Start with: the second movement, Alla siciliana, where "The Lily of the West" appears per Wikipedia.

Recording: Kenneth Schermerhorn / Nashville Symphony Orchestra / Naxos 8.559139.
