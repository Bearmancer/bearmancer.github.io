Ark Nova

Rules for your first game · 1–4 players, 90–150 minutes per Capstone Games

The Game in One Minute

You run a modern zoo, building enclosures, bringing in animals and backing conservation projects that protect species, which is how the rulebook introduces it. Two numbers matter. Appeal comes from your animals and pays you money at every break, while conservation comes from projects and brings bonuses along the way. Because the two counters travel toward each other, the game ends when they meet.

Goal and How You Win

You are aiming for the best combination of appeal and conservation. The rulebook triggers the end whenever your two counters share a scoring area, or have passed each other, at the end of your turn or during a break. The remaining turns depend on when that happens.

  • Triggered at the end of your turn: everyone else takes one more turn.
  • Triggered during a break: everyone takes one more turn, you included.
  • Highest score wins. A tie goes to whoever supported the most conservation projects.

Scoring needs one warning. The printed rulebook (version 1.2) subtracts a target number, the lowest appeal in your conservation scoring area, from your appeal. A newer method, described on Board Game Arena's help page and announced by the designer in a BoardGameGeek thread, simply adds the small number on your conservation space to your appeal, so that meeting exactly scores 100. This page uses the newer method. Your board may be an older print without those numbers, in which case the printed method applies, so look at your conservation track before you start.

How a Turn Works

Ark Nova has no fixed rounds. You take turns clockwise, and each turn is one action chosen from your five action cards.

  1. Pick one of your five action cards.
  2. Do that action at the strength printed over the slot the card sits in, from 1 to 5.
  3. Move the card to slot 1 and slide the cards that were to its left one slot to the right.

The rulebook describes this shift, and its consequence is that the card you just used becomes your weakest while the ones you leave alone drift toward strength 5. Choosing which action to hold back is therefore the main timing decision of the game.

  • X-tokens add 1 strength each, you decide how many to spend before you act, and you can hold at most 5.
  • If you cannot or do not want to use any of the five actions, you may take an X-token instead.
  • Upgrades flip a card to its stronger side, and the official FAQ says the new side works from your next action onward.

The Break

Some actions push the break token along its track, and when it reaches the last space a break happens after that turn. The rulebook's break page lists what everyone then does.

  1. Discard down to 3 cards in hand, or 5 with the right university.
  2. Return association workers to the notepad on your map.
  3. Discard the two lowest display cards and refill the display.
  4. Take income from your appeal space, your kiosks and any income icons.

Afterwards the break token returns to its starting space.

The Actions

There are five actions plus a fallback, and the strengths below are for the plain, unupgraded side.

Animals

  • Play animal cards from your hand into empty enclosures.
  • At strength 5 you can play two animals, as the rulebook's example shows, while the starting-deck guide plays a single animal at strength 2.
  • For each animal, meet its conditions, pay its cost, place it in an enclosure big enough and take its appeal and effects.
  • A partner zoo on the animal's continent cuts its cost by 3 per matching continent icon.

Build

  • Build one building up to the action's strength in size, at 2 money per space.
  • Choose a kiosk, a pavilion, a standard enclosure or a petting zoo.
  • Place it next to something you already built, never on water or rock.

Kiosks earn money at every break, and a pavilion gives 1 appeal once.

Cards

  • Move the break token 2 spaces, then draw from the deck.
  • The table on the card sets how many cards you draw, up to 3, and you may then have to discard one.
  • At strength 5 you may instead snap one card from the display.

Association

  • Place workers on the association board to do one task.
  • Gain 2 reputation at strength 2, take a partner zoo at strength 3, take a university at strength 4, or support a conservation project at strength 5.
  • Repeating a task that already has your worker on it costs 2 workers instead of 1.

The rulebook gives the tasks and their strengths, and the starting-deck guide uses strength 3 for the partner zoo and 5 for a conservation project in its first-turn steps.

Sponsors

  • Play one sponsor card from your hand, provided its level does not exceed the action's strength.
  • Or advance the break token that many steps and take that much money.

X-Token (Fallback)

  • Take 1 X-token whatever the strength.
  • Still move the action card to slot 1.

One Easy Strategy for Your First Game

The simplest route is the publisher's own beginner plan. The starting-deck guide hands each player a ready-made deck, so you skip the card-picking decision and follow a short list of first moves. It suggests Map A, with the Animals card in slot 1 and the Cards card in slot 2. The deck below is the guide's Player 1 deck.

  • Animals: Mantled Guereza, Rock Monitor, Slow Worm.
  • Sponsor: Herpetologist.
  • Base conservation project: Africa.

The guide's steps before the first break run in this order.

  1. Association at strength 3 or more: take the Africa partner zoo.
  2. Sponsors at strength 4 or more: play Herpetologist, and gain 3 money because the card itself has a reptile icon.
  3. Build at strength 2 or more: a 2-space enclosure for 4 money, next to your kiosk and a rock space.
  4. Animals at strength 5: play two animals, the Guereza for 10 money and the Rock Monitor for 9, then gain 3 money from Herpetologist.

After the first break, use Association at strength 5 to complete the Africa project and mark it with one of the upper four player tokens on your map, which unlocks an income. When an action card is in the wrong slot for the step you want, the guide says to play your Cards action in between.

Easiest Scoring Path

Scoring follows directly from the strategy above, since animals raise your appeal and the Africa project raises your conservation.

  • Play animals to add appeal, which also pays money at every break: on appeal space 7, you take 11 money.
  • Finish conservation projects to move up the conservation track, which pays one-time effects at 2, 5, 8 and 10 points.
  • Keep both counters closing on each other, since meeting is what ends the game.

The rulebook finishes the game with two players. Blue holds 16 conservation points, and the lowest appeal in that scoring area is 76, while Blue has 72 appeal. Red holds 18 conservation points, where the lowest appeal is 70, and Red has 79 appeal. The printed method subtracts the target number: Blue is 4 short at −4 victory points, and Red is 9 beyond at +9.

The newer method instead adds the small number on your conservation space to your appeal, and those numbers are set so that meeting scores exactly 100. The two methods then agree: the newer score is the printed victory points plus 100, so Blue lands 4 below 100 and Red 9 above it, with the 13-point gap unchanged. If your conservation track shows no small numbers, use the printed method.

Before that tally, each final scoring card adds up to 4 conservation points and any hourglass icon pays out. Where the two collide, the official FAQ settles it: "You lose the appeal before you gain the conservation points."

Changes for 4 Players

  • Put the 4 topmost base conservation projects face up below the association board, where other player counts use 3, as the rulebook's setup says.
  • Start the break token on the space for 4 players, and return it there after each break.
  • Give each player a different starting deck, as the starting-deck guide offers four of them, which also supplies four different base projects.
