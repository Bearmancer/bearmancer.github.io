Eight terms, each in one or two sentences. Use these senses in every lesson so fascism never drifts into generic insult.

Terms

Fascism

Italian-born movement of national rebirth through violence, founded in Milan in 1919 and in power from 1922 (Britannica, Fascism; USHMM, Fascism-1). Avoid: tyranny, dictatorship, right-wing.

Nazism

German racial variant of fascism whose core is Aryan rebirth through annihilation of Jews and others (USHMM, Nazi Racism). Avoid: fascism-in-general, authoritarianism.

Bolshevism

Faction formed in the 1903 party split that seized power in 1917 and renamed itself Communist in 1918 (Britannica, Bolshevism). Avoid: socialism, Marxism-at-large.

Leninism

Lenin’s doctrine of a vanguard party bound by democratic centralism to lead revolution (Britannica, Leninism). Avoid: Stalinism, totalitarianism.

Stalinism

Stalin’s 1929–53 system of leader cult, purges, and planned economy that claimed to strengthen the state (Britannica, Stalinism). Avoid: Leninism, fascism.

Totalitarianism

Broader label for rule claiming all life falls inside ideology, party, and terror, associated with Hannah Arendt’s 1951 study and with the six-trait model Friedrich and Brzezinski set out in 1956 (Britannica, Totalitarianism; Wikipedia, Totalitarianism). Avoid: fascism, dictatorship-alone.

Palingenesis

Griffin’s core of fascism: the myth of a fallen nation reborn through struggle and cleansing, the idea he named palingenetic ultranationalism in 1991 (Wikipedia, Palingenetic ultranationalism). Avoid: revival, reform, renewal-talk.

Generic Pejorative

Orwell’s December 1944 warning sense: fascist used to mean little more than bully (Orwell, What is Fascism?). Avoid: analysis, definition, history.
