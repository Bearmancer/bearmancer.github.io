Lesson 0002 · How a Name Became an Insult

Meaning Lost 1928-2025: From Social-Fascism to Bowie

Chapter 2 of 3

Lesson 0001 gave you the Italian spine. This lesson shows how the word escaped it: Communists, journalists, and novelists stretched fascist until Orwell called it almost meaningless, scholars rebuilt minimums, and recent debate saturated it again. Then David Bowie shows why the stretch matters in real life.

One Win · Insult Is Not Regime

Calling someone fascist can warn, smear, or joke. None of those acts proves a regime exists. Keep insult apart from regime and you can hear Bowie, Nixon, and every cable panel without confusion.

1. The Stretch Begins, Late 1920s to 1939

The Comintern branded its social democratic rivals as social fascists, treating them for a time as the most dangerous form of fascism1. That move turned fascist into a factional cudgel inside the left before it became an American insult. The widening is confirmed in the record: on 18 September 1939 a New York Times editorial declared that "Hitlerism is brown communism, Stalinism is red fascism"1. Brown and red, one menace. Precision was lost before the war was a year old.

2. Orwell Calls the Bluff, 1944-1946

In December 1944 Orwell wrote in Tribune that fascism had become almost entirely meaningless, used for little more than the word bully2. In April 1946, in Politics and the English Language, he tied dead metaphors and ready-made phrases to dishonest politics3. Read the two together and they are one argument in two parts: the 1944 column diagnoses the insult, the 1946 essay explains the machinery that keeps it alive. Neither piece names an American regime.

3. Scholars Rebuild, 1951-2004

Hannah Arendt in 1951 grouped Nazism and Stalinism under one totalitarian logic4. Friedrich and Brzezinski in 1956 listed six traits: ideology, one party, terror, media monopoly, arms monopoly, planned economy24. From 1991 to 2004 the minimums returned. Griffin's 1991 study made national rebirth the core of the definition19. Payne defines fascism by three concepts at once: anti-liberal, anti-communist, and anti-conservative20. Paxton's 2004 study examined how fascist movements take power and what they do with it21. Each minimum says what Orwell wanted: reserve fascist for regimes of national rebirth through violence, not for rivals you dislike. The rebuilding is partially correct as a rule and imperfect as a test, since the scholars never settled on one definition.

4. Saturation, 2018-2025

Five recent works ask whether fascism fits a present danger, each from a different angle: Jason Stanley on fascist rhetoric9, Sarah Churchwell on American usage of the word8, Bruce Kuklick on the word's century-long American career as an obsession and an insult7, the Cambridge survey edited by Gavriel Rosenfeld and Janet Ward5, and Federico Marcon's history of the word itself6. The answers differ. The effect compounds: every camp hears its own crisis in the word, and polling captures the blur, with an October 2024 ABC News survey finding half of Americans calling one candidate a fascist10. More talk, less agreement.

5. Case Study: Why Bowie Talked Fascism at All

In 1976 David Bowie toured as the Thin White Duke, a cold aristocratic persona1114. He also gave interviews musing about fascism and strong leaders, including a 1976 Playboy remark that Hitler was one of the first rock stars1213. In May 1976, arriving at London's Victoria Station, he was photographed with a raised arm that read to many as a Nazi salute13. He denied it and later blamed drug addiction and his own stage characters for the episode1213. What he intended is unverified; that the photographs circulated is not.

Britain in 1974-76 made the talk legible. Harold Wilson's ministry staggered through strikes and inflation, the sterling crisis forced a record IMF loan in 19761516, and the National Front marched through London streets, most violently at Lewisham in August 19772223. The interviews landed in a country that felt as if it were cracking.

Nixon Contrast · Insult Versus Regime

Richard Nixon announced his resignation on 8 August 1974 and left office the next morning1718. Popular memory calls him a liar and a paranoid; the record that matters here is narrower. No one-party state, no terror apparatus, no cancelled succession. A president can carry those vices and still leave office on schedule. Calling his government fascist is simply wrong, and that is exactly the insult-versus-regime line.

6. What to Keep From the Stretch

Two habits survive the whole century. First, ask whether the speaker is describing a feeling, a faction fight, or a regime. Second, check the claim against the lesson 0001 tests, since a rebirth myth plus an organised violent movement plus a seizure of power is a different object from a candidate you dislike. Keep insult and regime in separate pockets and most of this argument becomes tractable.
