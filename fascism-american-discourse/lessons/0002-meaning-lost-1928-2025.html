LESSON 0002 · HOW A NAME BECAME AN INSULT

Meaning Lost 1928-2025: From Social-Fascism to Bowie

Course Home Prev: 0001 This Lesson Next: 0003 Glossary

Lesson 0001 gave you the Italian spine. This lesson shows how the word escaped it: Communists, journalists, and novelists stretched fascist until Orwell called it almost meaningless, scholars rebuilt minimums, and recent debate saturated it again. Then David Bowie shows why the stretch matters in real life.

ONE WIN · INSULT IS NOT REGIME

Calling someone fascist can warn, smear, or joke. None of those acts proves a regime exists. Keep insult apart from regime and you can hear Bowie, Nixon, and every cable panel without confusion.

1. The stretch begins, 1928-1939

In 1928 the Comintern branded rival Social Democrats as social-fascists, turning fascist into a factional cudgel 1. Through the 1930s writers like Dorothy Thompson warned Americans about fascism at home while the label kept widening 1. By 18 September 1939 American papers already blurred the brown network and the red network into one menace, the brown-versus-red line later histories of the insult preserve 12. Precision was lost before the war started, and no single newspaper slug should carry the claim alone.

2. Orwell calls the bluff, 1944-1946

In December 1944 Orwell wrote in Tribune that fascism had become almost entirely meaningless, used for little more than bully 3. In April 1946, in Politics and the English Language, he tied dead metaphors and ready-made phrases to dishonest politics 4. Read the two together: the 1944 column diagnoses the insult, the 1946 essay explains the machinery that keeps it alive.

READ THEM IN ORDER

First the Tribune column on what fascism means, then Politics and the English Language. Five minutes on the first, ten on the second. Watch how bully does the work an argument should do.

3. Scholars rebuild, 1951-2004

Hannah Arendt in 1951 grouped Nazism and Stalinism under one totalitarian logic 5. Friedrich and Brzezinski in 1956 listed six traits: ideology, one party, terror, media monopoly, arms monopoly, planned economy 5. From 1980 to 2004 minimums returned: Griffin (1991) palingenesis, Payne (1995) triple negation, Paxton (2004) mobilizing passions 67. Each minimum says the same thing Orwell wanted: reserve fascist for regimes of national rebirth through violence, not for rivals you dislike.

4. Saturation, 2018-2025

Jason Stanley's work on fascist rhetoric, Sarah Churchwell's 2020 history of American fascism talk, Bruce Kuklick's 2022 Chicago commentary, Gavriel Rosenfeld and Janet Ward's 2023 Cambridge survey, and Federico Marcon's June 2025 intervention each ask whether fascism fits some present danger 8910. The answers differ. The effect compounds: every camp hears its own crisis in the word, and polling captures the blur, with an NPR survey finding 49 percent calling one candidate fascist and 22 percent saying the same of the other 11. More talk, less agreement.

5. Case study: why Bowie talked fascism at all

In 1976 David Bowie toured as the Thin White Duke, a cold aristocratic persona, and gave interviews musing that Britain could use a fascist leader 1213. At Victoria Station that May his raised-arm gesture photographed like a salute, a controversy he later blamed on cocaine, occult books, and character bleed, then retracted 1213. The standard references keep persona, remarks, salute row, and retraction together, which is why this lesson cites the Bowie career record beside the Thin White Duke account and checks both against the BBC obituary and the Guardian Bowie hub.

Britain in 1974-76 made the talk legible. Harold Wilson's ministry staggered through strikes and inflation, the IMF crisis loomed, and the National Front marched, the background preserved in the National Archives and the Hansard record and summarized daily by the BBC 14. Bowie said he saw a Britain cracking toward authoritarian temptation. He named fascism because street-level menace felt near, not because Westminster had fallen.

NIXON CONTRAST · INSULT VERSUS REGIME

Richard Nixon resigned in 1974: liar, incompetent in cover-up, paranoid on tape, but not a dictator. No one-party state, no terror apparatus, no cancelled succession. The record sits with the Nixon Library, the National Archives, and the Miller Center presidency study 15. A president can be all three vices and still leave office on schedule. That is exactly the insult-versus-regime line: fascist talk described Nixon often, yet no scholar of regimes calls his government fascist.
