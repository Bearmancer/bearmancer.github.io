LESSON 0001 · WHERE FASCISM ACTUALLY COMES FROM

Origins 1914-1932: From War Squads to Doctrine

Course Home This Lesson Next: 0002 Glossary

Your mission is to decode public debate. This lesson gives you the spine: fascism was born in Italy between 1914 and 1932, not as a generic insult but as a movement of national rebirth through violence. Hold this line and every later misuse gets easier to spot.

ONE WIN · PARTLY-RIGHT INTUITION, SHARPENED

Mussolini is Italy, Hitler is Germany, Stalin is Russia. That instinct is partly right and worth keeping. Sharpen it: Mussolini built fascism 1, Hitler built a German racial variant of it 2, Stalin built something else entirely, a Communist leader-cult regime 3. Fascism is the Italian-born one. The rest need their own names.

1. Interventionist roots, 1914-1915

Fascism starts not with Mussolini in power but with little war squads called fasci. On 5 October 1914 the Fascio rivoluzionario manifesto demanded Italy enter the war 4. On 11 December 1914 two interventionist groups fused into the Fascio d'azione rivoluzionaria 4. On 6 January 1915 that group issued a statute-program 4. On 24-25 January 1915 it held its first congress 4. By April 1915 the first men anyone called fascists in print were these interventionists, per Stanley Payne's history of the concept 5. The word meant pro-war leagues before it meant anything else.

WHY THE DATES MATTER

Five steps in four months. Manifesto, fusion, program, congress, then a name in common use. Fascism entered language as activism for war, not as a theory of the state. The state theory came seventeen years later.

2. From leagues to party, 1919-1921

On 23 March 1919 about one hundred veterans, syndicalists, and futurists met at San Sepolcro in Milan and founded the Fasci di combattimento, combat leagues 67. They marched, beat up opponents, and promised national rebirth. On 7 November 1921 the squads became a party, the Partito Nazionale Fascista (PNF) 8. League to party is the whole arc: street violence first, electoral machine second.

3. Taking power without a battle, 1922

On 28-30 October 1922 armed columns marched on Rome while Mussolini stayed in Milan 9. The king refused to declare martial law and invited Mussolini to form a government. No palace stormed, no battle won. Scholars call it a constitutional transfer under intimidation: legal form, coercive context. Remember that phrase. It stops anyone from calling every election win or street march a March on Rome.

4. The word that flipped, 1923-1925

Critics invented totalitario to attack Mussolini's claim to control everything. Then in 1925 Mussolini embraced it: tutto nello Stato, niente al di fuori dello Stato, all inside the state 1011. An insult became a boast. That flip is why later theory could group unlike regimes together: once totalitarian meant any all-claiming rule, fascist Italy, Nazi Germany, and Stalinist Russia could sit in one box even though their creeds differed.

THREE LENSES · 1991, 1995, 2004

Roger Griffin (1991): palingenesis, the myth of a fallen nation reborn through struggle 13. Stanley Payne (1995): triple negation, anti-liberal, anti-communist, anti-conservative at once 5. Robert Paxton (2004): mobilizing passions, crisis, victimhood, and violence as cure 14. Use Griffin for rhetoric, Payne for programs, Paxton for moods.

5. Doctrine at last, 1932

The Doctrine of Fascism, signed by Mussolini and drafted with Giovanni Gentile, finally gave the movement a creed: the state as spiritual, all-embracing, nothing outside it 1112. Read the short Hanover excerpts alongside the full SJSU text and you see the move clearly: squads in 1919, party in 1921, power in 1922, theory only in 1932. Action first, doctrine last.
