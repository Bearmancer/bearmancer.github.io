Lesson 0001 · Where Fascism Comes From

Origins 1914-1932: From War Squads to Doctrine

Home Chapter Index Glossary Cast Map

Chapter 1 of 3

Your mission is to decode public debate. This lesson gives you the origin story: fascism was born in Italy between 1914 and 1932, not as a generic insult but as a movement of national rebirth through violence. Hold this line and every later misuse gets easier to spot.

One Win · Fascism Is the Italian-Born One

Mussolini is Italy, Hitler is Germany, Stalin is Russia. That instinct is partly right and worth keeping. Sharpen it: Mussolini built fascism1, Hitler built a German racial variant of it11, Stalin built something else entirely, a Communist leader-cult regime13. Fascism is the Italian-born one. The rest need their own names.

1. Interventionist Roots, 1914-1915

Fascism starts not with Mussolini in power but with little war squads called fasci. The manifesto of the Fascio Rivoluzionario d'Azione Internazionalista is dated 5 October 1914 and demanded that Italy intervene in the war34. Two months later, on 11 December 1914, the Fasci d'Azione Rivoluzionaria was founded as an umbrella for pro-war activists3. It held its first meeting on 24 January 19153. In April 1915 Mussolini referred to his supporters as fascists for the first time, with the word in inverted commas3. The word meant pro-war leagues before it meant anything else.

Why the Dates Matter

Fascism entered language as activism for war, not as a theory of the state. The state theory came seventeen years later. One date sometimes attached to this sequence, a statute programme of 6 January 1915, does not appear in the sources checked and is left out as unverified.

2. From Leagues to Party, 1919-1921

On 23 March 1919 about one hundred veterans, syndicalists, and futurists met at Piazza San Sepolcro in Milan and founded the Fasci Italiani di Combattimento, the combat leagues5. They marched, beat up opponents, and promised national rebirth. On 7 November 1921 the squads became a party, the Partito Nazionale Fascista6. League to party is the whole arc: street violence first, electoral machine second.

3. Taking Power Without a Battle, 1922

On 28-30 October 1922 armed columns marched on Rome while Mussolini stayed in Milan7. The king refused to declare martial law and invited Mussolini to form a government. No palace was stormed and no battle was won. Scholars call it a constitutional transfer under intimidation: legal form, coercive context. Remember that phrase. It stops anyone from calling every election win or street march a March on Rome.

4. A Word for the State, Early 1920s

The word totalitario entered use in the early 1920s for the new fascist state. Britannica's account credits Mussolini with coining it, and records his own summary of the state he was building: all within the state, none outside the state, none against the state8. Whoever said it first, the word's career is what matters here. Once totalitarian could mean any all-claiming rule, fascist Italy, Nazi Germany, and Stalinist Russia could sit in one box even though their creeds differed13. That broadening is where the trouble starts.

Three Lenses · Griffin, Payne, Paxton

Roger Griffin in 1991 reduced fascism to palingenesis, the myth of a fallen nation reborn through struggle1. Stanley Payne in 1995 defined it by triple negation: anti-liberal, anti-communist, and anti-conservative at once2. Robert Paxton in 2004 listed mobilizing passions: crisis, victimhood, dread of decline, and violence as cure1. Use Griffin when a speech promises rebirth, Payne when a program rejects three enemies at once, Paxton when fear and street action do the persuading that policy cannot.

5. Doctrine at Last, 1932

The Doctrine of Fascism appeared in 1932 under Mussolini's name and gave the movement a creed: the state as spiritual, all-embracing, with nothing outside it9. Talk of that creed cannot leave out Giovanni Gentile, the philosopher of fascism, who served as minister of education in the first Fascist government and helped lay the foundations of the corporate state10. Read the full text and the sequence is plain: squads in 1919, party in 1921, power in 1922, theory only in 1932. Action first, doctrine last.

6. Compare, Do Not Conflate

MethodSquad violence, then one party1Annihilation, and Italy copies the 1938 Racial Laws12Purge, plan, cult of the leader8

Nazism is best read as a German racial variant of fascism, not its twin: racial annihilation sits at its core, while Italy added Racial Laws only in 1938 under German pressure1112. The distinction is confirmed in the comparison itself: two movements, two creeds, one borrowed name.

7. What the Comparison Buys You

Three payoffs, each usable in a live argument. First, the word applies to a movement with a rebirth myth, an organised violent wing, and a route to power, so a mere policy disagreement cannot fill it. Second, a regime can be murderous without being fascist, which is why Stalinism needs its own name13. Third, the word's descriptive force erodes each time it is stretched, and that erosion is the central problem the recent literature sets out to measure14. Name the regime, not the villain, and the argument stays honest.
