How to read the colours

Each kind of relationship has its own colour — that is the only difference between lines: every line is solid and the same weight. The colours are this workspace's own key, defined by the legend below (not a universal scheme; they stay stable across the chapter subgraph and this map). A link the fact-check flagged as allegation keeps a colour of its own rather than a different line style. The legend below is complete for this map, and every line is labelled in place.

Legend

  • Magenta — family. The one household link the chapter gives: Lyudmila Putina, Dresden 1985–90.
  • Teal — testimony & reporting. Who tells the story and who publishes it: Usoltsev's office-mate memoir; Schmidt-Eenboom publishing Lenchen's claims.
  • Blue — recruitment & agent-handling. Line N's pipeline: Putin's section forwarding East German candidates (Sonntag) for vetting.
  • Grey — a link the record leaves as allegation. Lenchen's reports on the household reaching the press; the Doris G. affair claim. Single-sourced throughout.
  • Red — rupture & abandonment. Gorbachev's reforms read as abandonment in East Berlin; the 1989 cascade that unseated Honecker.
  • Green — command & posting. Where Putin served (the Dresden residency, 1985–90); Gorbachev keeping the half-million Soviet troops in barracks in 1989.
  • Ochre — treaty & summitry. The INF treaty, Dec 1987: Gorbachev–Reagan negotiation as the chapter's glasnost product.
  • Olive — security liaison. The KGB residency and the Stasi district HQ a few blocks apart: cooperating services that also watched each other.

Who is who — the roster

The Dresden posting

  • Vladimir Putin — KGB officer on Line N in Dresden, 1985–90, under translator cover. Collects dossiers the FCD mostly discards; no valuable product on record. Faces the December 1989 crowd in his own account only.
  • Lyudmila Putina — Putin's wife; lives in Dresden 1985–90; second daughter Katerina born there 31 Aug 1986. The household Lenchen's reports describe.
  • Vladimir Usoltsev (documents: Agartanov) — Dresden office-mate; later memoirist (Sosluzhivets, 2003). Source of the "wasting its time on trifles" verdict and the rescued-contact-list colour.
  • Rainer Sonntag — Dresden-era recruitment case: neo-Nazi handled via Stasi channels (CORRECTIV archive reporting). Bounds Munger's "recruited nobody" as overbroad.
  • Dresden KGB residency — the villa at 4 Angelikastraße; Putin's upper-floor office. Reported upward to Karlshorst (East Berlin).
  • Stasi (MfS), Dresden — district HQ a few blocks from the KGB villa; stormed by crowds 5 Dec 1989 and disbanded shortly after.
  • Soviet Army, Dresden — tank regiment across the river. Putin says he called for guards and was told Moscow was silent; Gorbachev's standing order kept the troops in barracks.

The empire around them

  • Mikhail Gorbachev — Soviet leader 1985–91. Perestroika plus glasnost at home; the December 1988 UN signal abroad; refuses to intervene in 1989 and keeps the troops in barracks.
  • Erich Honecker — East German leader to Oct 1989. Reads the reforms as abandonment; the 1989 cascade unseats him.
  • Ronald Reagan — US president; signs the INF treaty with Gorbachev, Dec 1987 — the chapter's glasnost product.
  • Lech Wałęsa — Polish non-communist leader; the June 1989 semi-free elections carry his side into government and open the cascade year.

The 2011 flash-forward

  • ? Lenchen — alleged BND informant close to the Putin household; identity genuinely unknown. Teach as allegation: single-sourced via Schmidt-Eenboom.
  • Erich Schmidt-Eenboom — German intelligence journalist; published the Lenchen material Munger discusses. Cited by Munger; single-sourced — teach as allegation.
  • BND (West Germany) — West German foreign intelligence; reported recipient of the Lenchen material. Source of the 2011 claims at one remove.
  • Doris G. — alleged affair partner, daughter of a Stasi official. The video's account only; the relationship "didn't last long" and no child is mentioned.
