Putin: The Rise to Power · chapter 14 of 18

At Yeltsin's Court

Chapter 14 of 18 · 2:08:19–2:15:23

Lesson 08 · cast map · glossary · transcript slice

Putin climbs from property deputy to FSB chief in under two years while Yeltsin's young reformers pick a fight with the oligarchs over honest auctions — and lose. The chapter's arc is patronage plus scandal: the March 1997 reshuffle opens the Main Control Directorate door, Svyazinvest and the book affair close the reformers' window, and a failing presidency hands the secret police to the loyal apparatchik.

Every claim below carries its verdict inline beside the video's wording, with citations in §7. Names are given in canonical form; caption garbles (Chubais, Nemtsov, Svyazinvest, Shuyskaya Chupa, Kovalev) are silently corrected.

1. What matters — and what you can skip

Keep these four essentials:

  1. The postings chain is confirmed. August 1996 property deputy under Borodin, 26 March 1997 deputy chief of staff plus Main Control Directorate head, 25 May 1998 First Deputy, 25 July 1998 FSB. The video's "legal department of General Affairs" compresses the first title correctly in substance.
  2. The reshuffle is confirmed; the Chubais label is corrected. Chubais and Nemtsov both became First Deputy Prime Ministers in March 1997 — but Chubais was never an owner-oligarch. "Future oligarch" for him is the video's error; later he ran UES and Rosnano.
  3. Svyazinvest and the book affair are confirmed; the exits are corrected. The 25-percent-plus-one-share auction went honestly to the Oneksim consortium in July 1997, the losers retaliated through the $450,000 book advance, and neither reformer fell when the video says: Chubais was stripped of Finance but kept, Nemtsov lost Fuel and Energy first and the full post only with the March 1998 government.
  4. Health and the FSB landing are confirmed; motives stay hedged. The November 1996 bypass, the months of hospital, the December 1997 Stockholm wobble and the July 1998 appointment all check out. The strike-mishandling trigger for Kovalev's sack is unverified, the Lyudmila shopping colour is unfindable, and the 20-percent expenditure cut tied to Putin's directorate is unfindable — teach all three as the video's account, never as record.

What can be skimmed: the full concurrent-title list (the video waives it; so do we), the tsars-and-boyars close (flagged teaser for the next chapters, no verdict here), and the economics frame, which lives in the condensed companion rather than in fresh searching (see §7).

3. The climb — posts and the March 1997 reshuffle

"Putin originally came to Moscow as head of the legal department of the General Affairs office; he stayed only six months" — confirmed with the title compressed: in August 1996 he became deputy head of property management under Borodin, and on 26 March 1997 Yeltsin made him deputy chief of staff and head of the Main Control Directorate (Encyclopedia.com on the March 1997 double appointment). The second source states it directly: "On 26 March 1997 Yeltsin appointed Putin deputy chief of Presidential Staff and chief of Main Control Directorate" (Historydraft on the Staff and Directorate posts). August to March is roughly seven months, so "six months" is close enough to keep with a rounding note.

"Chubais suggested Putin get that job" — confirmed as a reported channel, hedged exactly as the record has it: brought to Moscow reportedly at Chubais's urging (Moscow Times on the Chubais channel), then appointed MCD head under Borodin and Chubais about seven months after arrival (Complutense study on the Borodin–Chubais posting). The Chubais-memo-beyond-"reportedly" detail is unfindable — teach it as reported, never as documented.

"In March 1997 Yeltsin initiated a new economic program and reshuffled the cabinet; Chubais, future oligarch, became Finance minister and Nemtsov was named First Deputy" — confirmed in structure, corrected on two points. Both men became First Deputy Prime Ministers under Chernomyrdin as young reformers: "In 1997 Yeltsin named Nemtsov first deputy PM alongside Chubais" (Britannica on the Nemtsov–Chubais pairing), and "In March 1997 Yeltsin reappointed Chubais first deputy PM under Chernomyrdin" (PBS on the Chubais reappointment). Correction one: Chubais was never an owner-oligarch — "future oligarch" is the video's error; he combined First Deputy with Finance from March 1997, lost Finance in November, kept the deputy post, and exited with the March 1998 government (Petersburg CEO profile on the dual post and dismissal). Correction two: Nemtsov served 17 March 1997 to 28 April 1998 alongside Chubais (Nemtsov dates and pairing).

"Positions were not always exclusive; you could hold more than one at the same time" — confirmed: the May to July 1998 stretch combined the First Deputy post with the regions commission and Security Council lineage (same FAS institutional history cited for the FSB years — see §7).

4. Taxes, Svyazinvest and the book that killed the reformers

"In 1997 Russia had two intertwined problems, taxes and the oligarchs; the state could not collect and the oligarchs did not feel like paying; Yeltsin insisted on tax reform and transparent privatisation" — confirmed as frame, carried from the condensed companion rather than fresh searching (condensed companion, chapters 12 and 14). "They cut state expenditures by 20 percent and vastly increased tax collection" is unfindable as a Putin-directorate result in five passes — teach the figure as rhetoric, never as record. The closest real adjustment came later and belongs to others, so the video's credit line stays unverified.

"The auctions of state properties were suddenly a lot more honest than before" — mixed: the honest-auction billing is confirmed as policy, the cleanliness is not. The sales were billed as a sell-to-the-highest-bidder break (RFE/RL on the honest-auction billing and the consortium bid), but the rigged-perception stayed and the debate continues — carry it as claimed break, not achieved clean.

"In July 1997 the government sold a 25 percent interest in Svyazinvest; Berezovsky and Gusinsky wanted it; the stake went to Oneksim Bank" — confirmed. The 25-percent-plus-one-share cash auction was set by Resolution 618 of 23 May 1997 (Svyazinvest auction terms); "Oneksimbank and Deutsche Bank won the 25 July auction for 25 percent plus one share" (RFE/RL on the July 1997 win). The consortium figure runs near two billion for army and state debt (same RFE/RL honest-auction report as above — see §7). "Both owned TV networks, ORT and NTV, which savaged the Communists in 1996 and helped Yeltsin win; they thought they were entitled" — confirmed: Berezovsky's bank held 38 percent of ORT while Gusinsky held NTV with Segodnya, Itogi and Ekho (RFE/RL on the ORT and NTV holdings); NTV people "advised Yeltsin PR" in 1996 (Nieman on the NTV campaign role); Media-Most plus LogoVAZ controlled two of the three channels (Monde diplomatique on the two-channel control). The Cannes pre-meet and the press-war threat are confirmed: Potanin recalls that if the auction did not end in victory the losers "would retaliate through press war" (Forbes on the Cannes summit pact).

"A publisher owned by Oneksim paid Chubais and friends a $450,000 advance for a book on privatisation history — $90,000 each, a bribe front, so the honest-auction talk was bogus" — confirmed. The Tribune report gives the totals and the per-head split with the publisher inside the Potanin empire (Tribune on the $450,000 book and $90,000 shares); Boiko and Mostovoi were sacked while Chubais survived (LA Times on the sackings and the survivor); Segodnya was controlled by Uneximbank (Moscow Times on the Uneximbank–Segodnya link).

"This was the end for Chubais — in November 1997 he resigned as Finance minister; Nemtsov got the boot in early 1998" — corrected on both exits. Chubais offered resignation and Yeltsin rejected it: stripped of Finance, kept as First Deputy (same Tribune report as above — see §7); Zadornov replaced him at Finance (Jamestown on the Finance replacement); he was "retained" First Deputy on 20 November 1997 (RFE/RL on the retention). Nemtsov's fall compressed two steps into one: on 20 November 1997 he lost Fuel and Energy but kept the deputy post (RFE/RL on the Fuel and Energy strip); the full government went in March 1998 and he returned as deputy under Kiriyenko from April to August (same Nemtsov dates page as above — see §7).

5. Money, Ozero and an ailing president

"Putin had ill-gotten gains, deals with foreign firms and gangsters since Petersburg, a dacha at Ozero with millionaire neighbours" — core confirmed, colour separated. Ozero was founded 10 November 1996 by Smirnov, Putin, Yakunin, the Fursenkos, Kovalchuk, Myachin and Shamalov at Solovyovka on Lake Komsomolskoye with a shared account (Ozero founders, site and account); the mechanism reading — parcels beyond salary, Twentieth Trust funds into the dacha, a half-million rebuild, the 1996 account as wealth-sharing without direct receipt — is the Dawisha account (Journal of Democracy on the diverted-funds chain). The "millionaire neighbours in 1997" projects later wealth backward — anachronism, corrected: the big figures date to the next decade, so teach neighbours-as-millionaires-in-1997 as the video's gloss. The foreign-firms-and-gangsters pointer reaches back to the Petersburg chapters and takes no verdict here.

"Skiing in Switzerland and Finland; Lyudmila buying designer goods in Germany and Israel until bags were refused and Putin cut her card" — unfindable in five passes: no source for the 1997 binge, the overweight bags, or the cutoff. The closest verifiable colour is thinner: a France and Biarritz vacation region with later properties (Politico on the vacation region and later properties), and the longer Ozero-to-palace wealth chain (USA Today on the Ozero-to-palace chain). Teach the whole shopping-and-skiing passage as uncorroborated colour — the record neither confirms nor supplies it.

"By late 1997 Yeltsin was not doing well — quintuple bypass the previous year, months in hospital, still drinking" — confirmed. The operation fell on 5 November 1996 (Tribune on the November 1996 operation); it was a seven-hour bypass with powers passed to Chernomyrdin (RFE/RL on the bypass and power transfer); ailments continued after (PBS on the continuing ailments). "Spent literally months in hospital" is confirmed as stated; double pneumonia kept him in hospital 8 to 20 January 1997 (RFE/RL on the January 1997 pneumonia stay), and the longer health record is tabulated separately (BBC health record).

"In Sweden in 1997 Yeltsin compared meatballs to Borg's face; maybe not pure drunkenness — drugs or a neurological balance disorder have been claimed" — confirmed with the video's hedge kept. The pool telling — talking nonsense, meatballs recalling Borg's face, balance lost after a single champagne — is the Nemtsov and Tregubova account (Yeltsin account of the Stockholm episode and the Staal neuro claim); the same telling runs in the Week's Clinton-tapes roundup (Week on the late-night memories). Stockholm dates to December 1997, with the Japan-nuclear and Norway-mix erratics and twelve days in Barvikha from 10 December on a viral infection (Independent on the December 1997 erratics and hospital stay). All three attributions — Nemtsov's strong-drugs-incompatible-with-alcohol, the alcohol reading, and Staal's secret 1999 neuro-operation for the balance wobble (same Yeltsin account as above — see §7) — stay live; teach all three, choose none.

6. Machinery — the FSB handover and the teaser

The mechanism this chapter establishes is scandal-as-selection: the reformers' honest-auction bid destroys the reformers, the presidency keeps failing, and the security service falls to the man who scared most easily — because, the video argues, he had the most to hide. Whether fear or loyalty moved Putin is the video's psychology; the appointment is record.

"Yeltsin had gone through several KGB–FSB chiefs, all ineffectual; by summer 1998 he tired of Kovalev; a minor strike mishandled while on vacation in Karelia triggered it; on 25 July 1998 he appointed Putin" — confirmed except the trigger. The decree removed Kovalev to "other work" and installed Putin; Putin was vacationing at Shuyskaya Chupa in Karelia and met Kiriyenko there (UPI on the vacation, the meeting and the replacement); no reasons were given and the vacation in the northwest was the dateline (LA Times on the reason-free firing); the nomination is tabulated in the institutional history (same FAS history as in §3 — see §7). The strike-mishandling trigger is unverified — no source in five passes. The press instead cites the Bliss technician arrest blunder and hotline, salary arrears (Kommersant on the salary-failure reading), and a Chernomyrdin-purge motive with ex-KGB advocacy (Moscow Times on the purge-motive reading). Teach the video's strike as unverified and the absence of a stated reason as the confirmed fact. "Several chiefs, all ineffectual" is gloss on real turnover — Barsukov, Stepashin, Kovalev — with the FSB Law of April 1995 and Decree 633 of June 1995 behind the renamed service (FSB turnover and statute account).

"At first he was not happy — the FSB seemed a dead end — but as the loyal apparatchik he had no choice" — unverified as psychology; the only citable texture nearby is Putin's own "walls are home to me" line. Flag it as the video's characterisation, and carry the concurrent-post line from §3 (First Deputy plus regions commission plus Security Council from October) as the confirmed part. The "renamed and slightly reorganised successor of the KGB" is broadly right (same FSB account as above — see §7).

"Once in power Putin solved the oligarch problem the way the old tsars handled the boyars" — no verdict; it is a flagged teaser for the coming chapters, carried as the video's frame. The video points to its own class page for the analogy (Munger class page behind the tsar analogy).

Career spine for reference: August 1996 property deputy, 26 March 1997 MCD, 25 May 1998 First Deputy, 25 July 1998 FSB (Putin career spine with Kudrin-to-Patrushev succession).
