The crown secured, the video takes attendance: parents gone before the presidency, a wife divorced, daughters kept out of sight, a mentor buried, rivals diminished or dead, and the predecessor eulogized with full Orthodox honors. The chapter's arc is the bill coming due in lives — every dated death confirmed, every rumor carried as rumor, and the video's adjectives kept as the video's, never as record.

Every claim below carries its verdict inline beside the video's wording, with citations in §7. This is an endings chapter: the family dates, the Sobchak death, the oligarch exiles and the Yeltsin funeral are all dated and sourced, while the side portraits — the 2 percent rating, the strategic-grounds objection, the gay rumor — stay marked unverified wherever no second carrier exists. Names are given in canonical form; caption garbles (Sobchak variants, Zyuganov, Zhirinovsky, Chubais, Berezovsky and Litvinenko slips aside) are silently corrected.

1. What matters — and what you can skip

Keep these four essentials:

  1. The family dates are confirmed; the motives are the video's. The father died in August 1999 and the mother in 1998, the divorce was announced in June 2013 with remarriage reported in early 2015, and both daughters' professions are confirmed in substance with Kremlin attribution kept. The traditionalist-friction reading of the marriage takes no verdict.
  2. Sobchak's death is confirmed inside a one-day range. The Paris flight, the dropped charges credited to Putin and the campaign-trail death sit at 19–20 February 2000, heart failure official with a poisoning rumor carried beside the autopsy. The Yeltsin-neutralization theory is unverified video texture.
  3. Every exile and poisoning is dated; every order-word is attributed. Berezovsky's hanging carries its no-third-party finding beside the murder rumors, the Litvinenko killing is banked as an inquiry's probably-approved finding rather than bare fact, and Chubais's flight is corrected from the day after to March 2022. The gay rumor and the strategic-grounds objection take no verdict.
  4. Yeltsin's farewell is confirmed except for its number. The 12-day admission, the April 2007 death, the national mourning and the first Orthodox burial in over a century are all sourced, while the 2 percent approval figure is taught as the video's number alone.

What can be skimmed: the Vera Putina detour after the May 2023 death is banked with its denial (the truth-value stays the video's), the daughters' genetics-and-AI project rumors after their confirmed professions (reported, never record), and the video's own promo and thanks, which decide nothing.

3. The family — dates confirmed, motives the video's

"Putin's father died in August 1999 just before he was named prime minister; his mother Maria had died the previous year 1998" — confirmed. "He died in August of 1999, the week his son was appointed prime minister of Russia. Putin's mother... died a year and a half before that" (biographical account with the parental dates), and "Maria Ivanovna Putina (née Shelomova; 1911–1998)" (family account with the lifespan). "Neither of them quite lived to see him achieve power as President" is confirmed in substance on those dates; "quite proud of their only surviving son" takes no verdict.

"A Georgian woman named Vera Putina... claimed that she was Putin's real mother but there was no evidence to back her story and it seems unlikely to be true; Vera Putina died in 2023 still sticking to her story" — partially correct, with the claim confirmed as alleged and the truth-value kept as the video's. "From 1999, stated that Vladimir Putin ('Vova') is her son" with death "in May 2023, at the age of 96" and burial "in Metekhi on 30 May" (Vera account with the claim and death), met by the spokesman's "The story is not true. It does not correspond to reality at all" (contemporary report with the denial). Teach May 2023 at age 96–97 — the 97 variant rides in the obituary press — and "no evidence / unlikely" as the video's assessment beside the denial.

"In 2013 the Kremlin announced that Vladimir and Lyudmila Putin were divorcing; she's kept a low profile since then, marrying her second husband a Russian businessman in 2015" — confirmed. "Announced that they have separated and that their marriage is over... married on July 28, 1983" (BBC with the June 2013 announcement), and "married 37-year-old Artur Ocheretny early last year" (Independent with the remarriage report). "She ran a foundation dedicated to the study of the Russian language" is confirmed — "curator of a fund that aimed to develop the Russian language" (Lyudmila account with the fund role) — while "chafed against her husband's traditionalist view of the role of women" is unverified: no carrier found, teach it as the video's reading of a guarded marriage.

"Maria Vorontsova Putin's eldest daughter became an endocrinologist... the younger daughter Katerina Tikhonova is a physicist and mathematician who has worked at Moscow State University" — confirmed in substance with attribution kept, since the Kremlin has never confirmed paternity. "Vorontsova is an endocrinologist... Tikhonova... heads a science and technology foundation affiliated with Russia's leading state university" (Moscow Times with the sanctions identifications), and "Vorontsova, an endocrinologist by training... appeared in person on a panel called Bioeconomy: Innovation and Bioproduction" (Novaya Gazeta with the forum appearance). "Rumored... genetic engineering" and "reportedly... AI" are partially correct: reported in the press, never record — teach both halves as attributed reporting, with the "too terrifying" line left as the video's shudder.

4. Sobchak and the loyal opposition — one range, one rumor each

"Sobchak fled to Paris in 1997... until they were dropped at Putin's insistence in 1999 he returned to Russia and campaigned... until Sobchak suddenly died on February 20th 2000; the official cause of death was a heart attack but there have been persistent rumors that he was poisoned" — confirmed with a one-day correction. "Died of a heart attack, aged 62, while drumming up electoral support for his former protégé... In November 1997, Sobchak fled to France fearing arrest on corruption charges... He returned home from France... after the corruption case was closed, a move credited to Putin" (Guardian obituary with the flight, return and death), and "When Sobchak returned to Russia from Paris last summer, Putin... was there to meet him at the St. Petersburg airport in a gesture of protection and support" (Los Angeles Times with the airport reception). Teach the death at 19–20 February — the 19th rides in the settled biography (Sobchak account with the 19th and the poisoning investigation) — and the cause as heart-failure official with the poisoning rumor beside it. "This campaign may have been drummed up by the Yeltsin administration to neutralize Sobchak" is unverified video theory, and "if he was murdered it was probably not by him" is the video's logic; both take no verdict.

"Zyuganov... is still alive and still the chairman of the Russian Communist Party... generally been the token opposition... often coming in a distant second... he supports the Russian war against Ukraine" — confirmed, with the adjectives kept as the video's. "Became its chairman" in 1993 and "placed a distant second behind Vladimir Putin in the March 2000 presidential election" (Zyuganov account with the chairmanship and result), and "Russia's Communist Party officially supports the war against Ukraine" (Meduza with the war-support finding). "Token," "not very influential" and "Putin does not seem to take him very seriously" are the video's judgments; they take no verdict.

"Zhirinovsky... remained in Russian politics until the end of his life... defended many of Putin's positions... arguing... that Putin didn't go far enough... widely rumored to have been secretly gay... died in 2022; Putin attended his funeral" — confirmed except the rumor. "Tolerated as a member of parliament largely because there was no chance that he, or the Liberal Democratic party... would ever take power" with shared "contempt for Ukraine" (Guardian obituary with the tolerated-nationalist portrait); "died after a long and serious illness... aged 75" (DW with the death notice); "Mourners... included Russian President Vladimir Putin" (Moscow Times with the funeral attendance). "Neither liberal nor democratic" is confirmed in substance by the same tolerated-opposition portrait; the sexuality rumor is unverified single-source gossip and takes no verdict.

5. Exiles and poisons — dated acts, attributed orders

"Chubais... remained the head of Russia's largest electricity utility until 2008, then moved to Rosnano... opposed Putin's invasion of Ukraine and wisely fled... he currently lives in Israel and is reportedly in poor health" — corrected in date, confirmed in substance. "Resigned as President Vladimir Putin's special envoy... due to the war in Ukraine... has also left the country" (AlJazeera with the March 2022 resignation and flight); "reports that Chubais was living in Israel" with hospitalization "in Italy for a rare immunity disorder" (RFE/RL with the Israel sighting and illness report); "head of state nanotechnology firm Rusnano... which he had run since 2008" until replacement in December 2020 (Moscow Times with the Rosnano tenure). Teach the flight as March 2022, never the day after, and "opposed Putin's appointment as prime minister on strategic grounds" as unverified: no carrier found. "Not a member of Putin's trusted inner circle" is the video's placement; it takes no verdict.

"Berezovsky... fell out with Putin after he was in power most publicly over Putin's handling of the Kursk submarine disaster... sold off most of his assets and the Kremlin eventually got control of his networks; he went into exile in Great Britain... went into business with Neil Bush... died in Berkshire England in 2013; supposedly he hanged himself... persistent rumors that he was murdered... tried to mend fences with Putin at the very end" — confirmed. "When the Kursk submarine sank... Berezovsky's ORT TV channel slammed Putin... Berezovsky was told to sell his TV station or go to jail. Instead, he chose exile" (Guardian review with the Kursk rupture); "sent a letter to Putin asking permission to return... admitted that he had made a lot of mistakes, asked forgiveness" (CNN with the mend-fences letter); "Berezovsky... had been an investor in Bush's Ignite! program since at least 2003" (Neil Bush account with the Ignite investment). "Cause of death is consistent with hanging... nothing to indicate a violent struggle" (AlJazeera with the post-mortem) with "no evidence to suggest anyone else was involved" (Guardian with the police finding) — teach the hanging confirmed and the murder talk as rumors beside the denial.

"Vladimir Litvinenko the director of St Petersburg Mining University and the man who wrote Putin's fraudulent dissertation is still alive and still in that office; he managed Putin's presidential campaigns in 2000 and 2004" — corrected from allegation to allegation: the facts confirmed, "fraudulent" kept as the video's word. "Has been the rector of Saint Petersburg Mining University since the 1990s... oversaw Vladimir Putin's dissertation work in 1996... was Putin's political campaign manager in 2000 and 2004" (rector account with the dissertation and campaigns); "Vladimir Stefanovich Litvinenko is... Rector of the St. Petersburg Mining University" (official university page with the current rectorship); the plagiarism mechanics in the Brookings study (Brookings with the dissertation forensics). Teach alleged plagiarism and alleged ghostwriting, never bare fraud.

"Alexander Litvinenko... accused Putin of trying to assassinate Berezovsky and later of masterminding the Moscow apartment bombings, fled Russia in October 2000... settled in London... poisoned with radioactive polonium on Putin's orders while having drinks at the Pine Bar of the Millennium Hotel... died on November 23rd 2006" — corrected and confirmed. "Accuse his superiors of ordering him to assassinate the businessman Boris Berezovsky" with the 1999-bombings book charge (Independent obituary with the accusations); "travelled to Turkey" in October 2000 and "asked for political asylum at Heathrow Airport during the transit stop on 1 November 2000" (defector account with the flight) — teach the flight as October–November 2000. "Fatal dose of polonium-210 on the first of the month" at the Pine Bar (BBC with the CCTV and dosage reconstruction), and the Owen inquiry's "probable... that Mr. Putin and his spy chief at the time, Nikolai Patrushev, had approved an operation to kill Mr. Litvinenko" (CNN with the inquiry finding). "On Putin's orders" is taught only as that probably-approved finding; the 23 November death and the roughly three-week interval are confirmed on the same carriers.

6. Machinery — the farewell as statecraft

The mechanism this closing chapter establishes is succession-as-eulogy: the man who made Putin acting president is buried with honors that retroactively dignify the handover. "At the time of his resignation... Boris Yeltsin's approval rating among the Russian public was 2%" is unverified: the figure rides in the video alone with only a flagged tertiary echo — teach it as the video's number, never record. "Exhausted and sick he retired to his home in Moscow and kept low profile... continued to take foreign trips... together with his former communist rival Gorbachev was a mild critic of Putin" is partially correct: "Yeltsin appeared rarely in public, except for an occasional official ceremony, holiday reception or tennis tournament" (PBS with the low-profile retirement); the mild-critic texture with Gorbachev stays the video's.

"Yeltsin's final hospitalization lasted 12 days; on April 23rd 2007 he died not surprisingly of congestive heart failure; Putin declared a national day of mourning... Yeltsin was the first Russian leader since Tsar Alexander III to have a formal contemporaneous funeral in the Russian Orthodox Church" — confirmed. "Died of cardiac arrest on 23 April 2007, twelve days after being admitted to the Central Clinical Hospital" (death account with the admission and date); "died of heart failure at the age of 76" (VOA with the cause); "issued a decree declaring 25 April a day of national mourning" (Kremlin with the mourning decree); "first orthodox church-sanctioned funeral for a Russian head of state in more than a century" (Guardian with the funeral) and "first Russian head of state to be buried in a church ceremony since Emperor Alexander III, 113 years prior" (same death account as above — see §7). The closing thesis — context first, Putin to understand the world — and the promo lines take no verdict; they are the video's frame, and the lesson keeps them that way.
