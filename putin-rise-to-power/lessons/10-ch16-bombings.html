The Apartment Bombings

Chapter 16 of 18 · 2:22:31–2:29:24

Lesson 10 · cast map · glossary · transcript slice

Four apartment blocks go down in twelve September days, a fifth device fails in Ryazan, and a new prime minister gets his war and his image in the same month. The chapter's arc is attribution under fire: the official Chechen-trail account and the FSB-did-it charge both survive the evidence, and what is certain is only the use — retaliation, a second Chechen war, and a terrorist-fighter persona that carries into the 2000 election.

Every claim below carries its verdict inline beside the video's wording, with citations in §7. This is a contested chapter: no disputed toll appears as a single number — every one is a range with its dissent — and the video's own beliefs are attributed as the video's, never as record. Names are given in canonical form; caption garbles (Guryanova, Ryazan, Gochiyaev, Chechnya, Dagestan, Karachay, polonium, Tonkin) are silently corrected.

1. What matters — and what you can skip

Keep these four essentials:

  1. The sequence is confirmed; every toll is a range. Manezh mall on 31 August, Buynaksk on 4 September, Guryanova on 9 September, Kashirskoye on 13 September, Volgodonsk on 16 September, Ryazan on 22–24 September. Killed ranges: Buynaksk 62–64, Guryanova 94–106, Kashirskoye 118–124, Volgodonsk 17–19, four-block total 293–307+. The video's single figures (106, 307) are corrected to ranges; its Manezh fatality is unattested.
  2. The official case is confirmed as the official case — and the plot charge is confirmed as a live allegation. The 2002 finding names the ethnic-Karachay Gochiyaev under Khattab's planning, in retaliation for Dagestan; Gochiyaev stays at large while two associates drew life terms in a closed 2004 trial. Against it stand the Satter/Hudson FSB-responsibility case, the Litvinenko–Felshtinsky book, and the seized-evidence record — met by the Kremlin rejection and the Pribylovsky staged-find reading. Teach both, choose neither.
  3. Ryazan is confirmed as events, contested as substance. Sacks, timer set for the small hours, evacuation, FSB cards flashed, release after the Moscow call, Patrushev's next-day exercise-and-sugar announcement — all confirmed. Hexogen or sugar is unresolved: the Interior Ministry's hexogen line and Tkachenko's live-bomb account against the sugar announcement and the 2002 no-traces finding, with the alcohol-cleaning rebuttal favoring neither side conclusively. Three readings of intent (live plot, Vympel exercise, staged find) stay live.
  4. The political use is confirmed; the pre-plan reading rides alongside. The 23 September Grozny raids blame Chechen commanders for the blasts, the 24 September outhouse line mints the persona, and the war runs August 1999 to April 2009 with Putin's ratings climbing from meagre — yet Stepashin's March-decision admission means the invasion may have needed no bombs at all. The video's Tonkin and Reichstag analogies, its ruthlessness judgment and its "probably did not do it" belief take no verdict; they are carried as the video's frame.

What can be skimmed: the shopping-mall opener after its date and injuries are banked (the fatality detail is video-only), the "thousands at risk" Ryazan estimate (sourced 250 tenants beside it, neither chosen), and the litmus-test sociology of Putin opinion, which is the video's commentary, not a claim.

3. The September sequence — mall, blocks and the toll ranges

"Here on August 31st 1999 a bomb exploded near the food court; one person was killed and more than 30 others injured" — partially correct: the date, place and order are confirmed while the fatality is not. "At evening rush hour, a bomb exploded in the amusement arcade of the Manezh Square shopping complex of Moscow" with "at least 29 people were injured" and a roughly 300-gram device per the FSB (bombings account with the Manezh and trial detail); the 2009 trial tied the mall blast to long sentences (same account as above — see §7). A dedicated counter-search found no source for any death — teach the injuries confirmed and the single death as the video's figure only.

"A nine-story apartment block on Guryanova Street in Moscow; this bomb killed 106 people and injured 249" — corrected to a range, never a single number: the settled account gives 106 killed with early reports at 93 dead and 249 injured (same bombings account as above — see §7), against "more than 100" (RFE/RL on the 2019 toll reconstruction) and "ninety-four people died" (RFE/RL on the 2002 toll reconstruction). Teach 94–106 killed, about 249 injured, with the dissent inline. "These attacks, which collectively killed 307 people" is likewise corrected to the range 293–307+: "a total of 293 people dead" (Moscow Times timeline with the 118 Kashirskoye figure) against the 307 infobox figure (same bombings account as above — see §7) and "more than 300 people died" (same 2019 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7). The Kashirskoye toll inside that total is itself a range, 118–124: 118 (same Moscow Times timeline as above — see §7) against 119 (same 2019 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7) and 130 (same 2002 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7). Buynaksk on 4 September sits at 62–64 killed ("sixty-two people died", same 2019 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7, against 64 in the bombings account) and Volgodonsk on 16 September at 17–19 ("seventeen people died", same 2019 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7).

4. The official case — Gochiyaev, Khattab and the FSB-plot charge

"The Yeltsin administration was quick to blame them on Chechen separatists, and eventually they pointed fingers at a man called Gochiyaev, who interestingly was not a Chechen but a member of the Karachay ethnic group, as the mastermind" — confirmed as the official attribution, with the dissent riding alongside. "Chechen militants were blamed for the bombings, but denied responsibility, along with Chechen president Aslan Maskhadov," and per the prosecutor's office the bombings were "executed under command of ethnic Karachay Achemez Gochiyayev and planned by Ibn al-Khattab and Abu Omar al-Saif" (same bombings account as above — see §7); the closed 2002 finding reads the same motive — organized by Gochiyaev, ordered by Khattab and al-Saif "in retaliation for the Russian counteroffensive" into Dagestan (Second-War account with the official finding). "Gochiyaev in fact called police to report two additional bombs, which were found in this apartment building and defused before they went off" — confirmed: on 13 September he reported the Borisovskiye Prudy and Kapotnya devices, both found and defused (same bombings account as above — see §7). He remains the fugitive at the center: "has not been arrested; he is still at large if alive," while Krymshamkhalov and Dekkushev were "sentenced to life imprisonment in January 2004, after a two-month secret trial held without a jury" (suspects account with the trial record); the video's "two associates gave partial confessions" is confirmed only as trial-record claim per the official account (same suspects account as above — see §7), never independently verified.

"Days after this incident a Moscow tabloid conjectured that the attacks were not the work of Chechens or their allies at all but had been engineered by the FSB ... Putin, who had just been appointed prime minister, was supposed to have been involved" — confirmed as a live allegation with its counter-charge inline. The strongest accusation-side form holds "the evidence is overwhelming that the apartment-house bombings ... were carried out by the FSB," paired with the transcript-verified Seleznev early announcement of Volgodonsk three days before it happened (Hudson/Satter case with the Seleznev transcript); the book form "allege that it was actually the FSB, and not terrorists, who were responsible," with Ryazan as the first doubt (RFE/RL on the Litvinenko–Felshtinsky book). The counter-search returns three weights on the other pan: the Kremlin rejection — "never independently proven and were strongly rejected by Putin and Kremlin officials" (PBS with the unproven verdict); the cleared-rubble and blocked-inquiry record with no Chechen claim of responsibility (same 2002 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7); and the Pribylovsky minority reading that the FSB left powder to be "found" for a morale-boosting win rather than a live bombing (same 2002 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7). "The materials used in the bombs found at Ryazan were materials that the FSB was generally in charge of guarding" is confirmed only as Satter-attributed allegation: hexogen from "one plant in Perm Oblast for whose security the central FSB is responsible," with the claim that the FSB "changed the story about the type of explosive" when the disappearance grew hard to explain (same bombings account as above — see §7) — met by the official 10 September 1999 mix line of hexogen plus TNT at about 350 kilograms TNT-equivalent, since pure hexogen is too dangerous to transport (RFE/RL Ryazan reconstruction with the Kruglov analysis).

"One of them was Alexander Litvinenko, who wrote a book on the subject and whom Putin murdered in 2006 with polonium" — corrected to attributed finding, never bare fact and never proof of the bombings. The book half is the allegation above (same RFE/RL book report as above — see §7); the murder half reads "died from acute radiation syndrome on 23 November 2006" after the 1 November polonium-210 tea, with the inquiry finding the FSB operation "probably approved by Mr Patrushev and also by President Putin" (BBC on the inquiry findings). "Litvinenko's murder has sometimes been cited as proof by itself that Putin did it" is the video's warning against its own side's logic — carried as the video's caution, with the finding's "probably" doing the load-bearing work.

5. Ryazan — sacks, timer, cards, and hexogen-or-sugar

"On September 22nd another whistleblower in the suburb of Ryazan, who had noticed a strange car in front of his apartment building, called police, where they discovered more explosives and a timed detonator; if this one had gone off it could have killed thousands" — confirmed as events, unverified as number. Residents spotted the trio and the doctored plates, police found "three large sacks ... hexogen, attached to a timer and detonator," evacuated the building, and only then — two days later — heard the exercise claim (Jamestown/Satter account of the Ryazan sequence); "the timer was set for the early morning, to kill as many people as possible, as was the case" in Moscow (same RFE/RL Ryazan reconstruction as above — see §7); the bomb man Tkachenko tested the sacks with his MO-2 analyser, got a hexogen positive, and read the detonator as set for the small hours — "it was a live bomb ... I was in a combat situation" (Guardian with the Tkachenko account). "Three suspects were identified ... two were arrested and found to be carrying ID cards issued by the FSB" is confirmed in substance with the count hedged: "arrested by local police they flashed identity cards from the FSB," released only after Moscow intervened, with the exercise announcement two days later (same Guardian account as above — see §7); the three-agent planting with arrest-and-release is independently given (same Jamestown account as above — see §7). "Could have killed thousands" stays the video's estimate: the sourced occupancy is "many of the 250 tenants of the 13-storey block" (same Guardian account as above — see §7) — teach 250 beside "thousands," choosing neither.

"The involvement of FSB people in the Ryazan thing, where the bombs failed to go off, might have been an FSB exercise" — contested, with three live readings and the lab dispute inline. For hexogen: the Interior Ministry press center "officially talked about the hexogen in the bags" on 23 September with an Article-205 terrorism case and televised sketches (same RFE/RL Ryazan reconstruction as above — see §7), and the resident's "yellowish noodles" reads as TNT flakes amid sugar-like hexogen crystals (same reconstruction as above — see §7). For sugar: Patrushev's next-day "training" with "only sugar," and the Deputy Prosecutor's 2002 "no traces of any explosives" line (per the bombings account — see §7). The rebuttal that keeps the dispute alive: the cleaning story fails — the equipment "is never cleaned with alcohol" and week-old hexogen on unwashed hands is implausible — and a soldier assigned to guard "sugar" sacks near Ryazan tasted foulness that "turned out to be hexogen" (same Jamestown account as above — see §7). Intent then splits three ways: Satter's live-plot reading (same Hudson case as above — see §7), the Soldatov–Borogan Vympel-training-exercise reading (per the bombings account — see §7), and Pribylovsky's staged-find reading (same 2002 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7) — the lesson chooses none, as the video itself refuses to choose.

6. Machinery — use as selection

The mechanism this chapter establishes is use-as-selection: whether or not the attribution ever resolves, the bombings select the war and the war selects the man. "In September 1999" Putin calls "for swift retaliation against Chechnya; the second Chechen War began that month with an offensive launched shortly after the bombing incidents" — confirmed as act. "Russian warplanes twice bombed the airport in the Chechen capital Grozny ... the first big attack on the city," with Moscow accusing "guerrilla commanders in Chechnya of orchestrating the four devastating explosions" (Guardian on the 23 September raids); the war runs "from August 1999 to April 2009" (same Second-War account as above — see §7). The dissent rides in the same paragraph: the invasion "was planned and would have happened even if there had been no terrorist attacks," decided in March when Stepashin was premier (Norwegian Helsinki Committee with the Stepashin admission) — retaliation narrative and pre-plan reading, both live. "Even if Putin knew that the apartment bombings were not carried out by Chechens, two things can be true at the same time: one, that the FSB did not do it, and two, that he was perfectly happy to claim the Chechens did it in order to drum up support" — confirmed as analysis with both feet: the blame-assertion of 23 September and next-day Astana line "we will pursue them everywhere ... we will wipe them out in the outhouse" (same 2019 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7), the same "most memorable quote" with the war propelling "Putin to front ranks" (LA Times on the quote and the climb), and "meager poll ratings started to rise quickly" (same RFE/RL Astana report as above — see §7) beside "rode to an easy victory in presidential elections the following year" (same 2002 RFE/RL reconstruction as above — see §7). No approval number is taught; the rise is confirmed, the figures were never sourced.

"Personally I believe Putin probably did not do it, but I am unwilling to say that there is no doubt of that conclusion" — no verdict; it is the video's belief, attributed as such, and the lesson keeps it that way. The Tonkin and Reichstag analogies ("at least one of two purported attacks did undoubtedly occur" vs. "ginned up out of whole cloth, which is absolutely not what happened"; "the Nazis most likely did not cause the fire themselves but clearly did use it") take no verdict; they are the video's comparative frame. "The apartment bombings clearly did pave the way for Putin cementing his power" and the "tough terrorist fighter" image "carried him easily through the presidential election of 2000" are confirmed in substance (same LA Times account and RFE/RL reconstructions as above — see §7) with the numbers stripped out. Putin's 9 August arrival in the premiership, which the plot charge leans on, is itself confirmed — Yeltsin fired Stepashin and named the FSB head acting premier with the succession hint (RFE/RL on the 9 August appointment).
