The French Sovereign Debt Crisis

France's debt-to-GDP ratio reaching 120% pushed sovereign bond spreads over German Bunds to 175 basis points, their widest risk premium since the 2011 crisis.123

The Mechanics of European Sovereign Spreads: OATs vs. Bunds

To understand the sudden panic in European fixed income, one must examine how the Eurozone prices sovereign credit. Germany's 10-year government bonds (Bunds) represent the risk-free benchmark of Europe, anchored by constitutional deficit restrictions (the Schuldenbremse) and a pristine fiscal balance sheet. All other European government bonds trade at an interest rate spread—a risk premium—above German Bunds.

French 10-year sovereign bonds are called OATs (Obligations Assimilables du Trésor). Historically, French OATs traded almost synchronously with German Bunds, with spreads fluctuating tightly between 20 and 40 basis points. That stability has abruptly shattered.

The Spread Blowout: Widest Risk Premium Since 2011

France's debt-to-GDP ratio has reached 120%.12

The spread between French 10-year sovereign bonds and German 10-year Bunds widened by 16 basis points to 175 basis points.12

The risk premium on French 10-year sovereign bonds reached its highest level since the 2011 European sovereign debt crisis.12

A spread widening of 16 basis points in a single wave pushed French 10-year borrowing costs above 5.0%. At 175 basis points, France is paying its highest risk premium over Germany since the depths of the 2011 Eurozone sovereign debt crisis, when default contagion swept Greece, Portugal, and Ireland. In an unprecedented inversion, French sovereign borrowing costs briefly rose to exceed Italian BTP yields, historically considered vastly riskier.

Why It Happened: Chronic Deficits and Political Gridlock

The sudden rebellion of bond investors ("bond vigilantes") against French paper stems from three structural realities:

  • Runaway Public Expenditure: French public spending exceeds 57% of GDP—the highest ratio of any European economy. Persistent budget deficits exceeding 5.5% of GDP have breached the European Union's 3.0% Maastricht limit for years.
  • Snap Elections and Parliamentary Deadlock: Following snap legislative elections called by President Emmanuel Macron, the French National Assembly fractured into three mutually hostile blocs: the left-wing New Popular Front, the presidential center, and the right-wing National Rally. With no group holding an absolute majority, passing budget austerity, curtailing pension spending, or raising taxes is politically impossible. Any prime minister who introduces fiscal consolidation faces an immediate vote of no confidence.
  • The Absence of Currency Sovereignty: Unlike the United States, France does not control its own monetary press. Operating inside the Eurozone, France cannot unilaterally devalue its currency or force the European Central Bank (ECB) to monetize its debt. The ECB's Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI) requires strict adherence to EU fiscal discipline—the very thing the French parliament cannot deliver.

The Systemic Message: Bond Vigilantes Have Returned

Why did Steve Eisman dedicate central time to French sovereign debt during a discussion of artificial intelligence? Because France is the canary in the macroeconomic coal mine. It demonstrates that financial markets have re-established discipline over debt-burdened entities.

When even G7 sovereign nations face punishing yield spikes and credit rationing because their spending math does not work, the message to private markets is absolute: there is no backstop, no cheap bailout, and no return to zero rates. If sovereign debt pays 5%, speculative corporate AI infrastructure financed at 15% junk yields is living on borrowed time.
