The AI Infrastructure Debt Problem

Surging borrowing costs for AI cloud providers have elevated data center debt yields to 15%.12

The Shift to Heavy Industrial Capital Expenditure

For three decades, software development represented the ultimate capital-light enterprise: code written once could be replicated across millions of users at near-zero marginal cost, generating gross margins above 80%. Generative artificial intelligence has abruptly upended this model. Frontier AI models require gargantuan physical compute clusters, converting technology companies into heavy industrial consumers of raw electricity, industrial chillers, optical fiber, and silicon accelerators.

The Economics of Gigawatt-Scale AI Campuses

Developing a 1-gigawatt (1 GW) AI-optimized data center campus requires between $38 billion and $55 billion in upfront capital expenditure. Unlike traditional commercial real estate, where core structures depreciate over 30 to 40 years, AI hardware undergoes relentless technical obsolescence:

  • Compute Silicon: GPU accelerators and high-density servers represent approximately $21 billion to $25 billion upfront (roughly 60% of total annualized ownership cost). With cutting-edge accelerators replaced every 3 to 5 years, hardware depreciation creates an ongoing financial drag of approximately $8.5 billion annually per campus.
  • Power Distribution & Cooling: AI server racks draw between 30 kW and over 100 kW per rack—compared to 5 to 15 kW for conventional cloud servers. Upfront high-voltage mechanical switchgear, substations, and closed-loop direct-to-chip liquid cooling demand an additional $11 billion to $14 billion.
  • Networking Fabric: Ultra-low-latency InfiniBand and optical switches add roughly $5 billion.

The Neocloud Trap: CoreWeave's Yield Surge

CoreWeave's cost of borrowing surged with debt yields climbing from approximately 9% to 15%.12

Lacking the multi-billion-dollar cash cows enjoyed by Google, Meta, or Microsoft, specialized AI cloud providers ("neoclouds") like CoreWeave accumulated an estimated $50 billion to $52 billion in total debt and equipment lease obligations to finance cluster expansion. While CoreWeave highlights a multi-year contracted revenue backlog exceeding $104 billion, credit markets have grown alarmed by the extreme asset-liability duration mismatch.

When senior unsecured notes originally issued around 9.6% surged to 15%–16% in secondary trading, CoreWeave bond prices plunged to distressed levels between 75 and 80 cents on the dollar, while five-year credit default swaps escalated toward 1,000 basis points. At a 15% to 16% cost of debt, data center unit economics collapse: debt service absorbs all cash flows before silicon clusters can even break even.

Physical Constraints: Grid Queues and Regional Bottlenecks

Compounding financial pressures, physical infrastructure availability has become the binding constraint on expansion. Transmission interconnection queues span 3 to 5 years in North America and Western Europe. In key emerging data center hubs such as India—across Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru, Delhi-NCR, and Hyderabad—rapid AI workload deployment places severe localized stress on state distribution utilities (DISCOMs) and transmission corridors during peak afternoon solar-drop windows, forcing providers into expensive behind-the-meter generation.
