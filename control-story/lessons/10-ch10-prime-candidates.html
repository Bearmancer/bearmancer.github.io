1. What the chapter does

The Bureau auditions children for godhood, numbers them, and buries most of them. Dylan is P6 and Jesse is the one who got away as P7.

3. What matters — and what you can skip

Keep these essentials:

  • Dylan is P6 and Jesse is P7; the designations are documented.
  • Dylan killed agents, was isolated, and landed in the Panopticon.
  • Jesse grew up watched but free, the control to Dylan's test.

Can be skimmed:

  • Fates of P1 through P5 beyond what the files state.
  • Numbering trivia and collectible ordering.

4. The narrative

The numbering is the one hard fact in a soft file:

"Dylan and Jesse were dubbed P6 and P7."

The designations are confirmed in the ending breakdown, which adds the study design: Jesse lived free under watch while Dylan grew up in custody.

Dylan's file reads as promise curdling. Darling judged him a parautilitarian comparable to Northmoor, then watched him kill Roberts, then more agents, until isolation and the Panopticon cell. That chain is confirmed on Dylan's page.

The comforting idea that Jesse escaped by luck is corrected by the same coverage: the Bureau knew both siblings could hold the chair and kept Jesse as its unhandled spare. What the earlier candidates suffered stays unverified in this pass, and the program's founding charter is left for the Bureau historians.

5. The machinery

The program is a pipeline. Identify the gifted, raise them inside, bind the survivors to power, and file the rest.

Jesse breaks the pipeline by arriving unbureaucratized: no raising, no filing, only the binding.
