3. Narrative

After five years of rule, girls are "completely banned from education" and women are barred from most sectors of the economy. The ban wording is now confirmed against the 20 December 2022 decree itself (primary), consistent with rights baselines such as Human Rights Watch World Report 2025 on Afghanistan. About half of Afghan women leave home "only once or twice a month," travel without a male relative is prohibited, and weakened legal protections feed domestic violence and child marriage, with those mobility figures staying unverified on this chapter's evidence.

Tajiks, Uzbeks, and especially Hazaras face systematic exclusion, with land confiscation, targeted taxes, and direct attacks. Islamic minorities face forced conversion to Sunni Islam, while Sikhs, Hindus, Ahmadis, and other non-Muslims stand effectively expelled. That persecution pattern is confirmed in outline against the Amnesty International Afghanistan reporting, with the narration's specifics and any population-share reading quoted as the source's account, unverified as stated.

  • Education erased: the school ban as the starkest breach of the bargain.
  • Minorities squeezed: exclusion, confiscation, and expulsion down the hierarchy.
  • Country as accomplice: drought, flood, dead aquifers, and brutal cold deepen every wound.

The disaster passage states no casualty figure of its own.
