Movements and Fronts

Taliban — The fundamentalist movement ruling Afghanistan since retaking Kabul in 2021. The narration treats it as a single contractor that broke its bargain with the people.

Emirate — The Taliban's own name for its order, quoted from the leaked audio warning that the Emirate could collapse.

Supreme Leader — The unnamed ruler-by-decree in Kandahar. The narration never speaks a personal name, so no name is asserted here.

National Resistance Front (NRF) — The exile-led, pro-democracy resistance of Ahmad Massoud, rooted in northern minority networks. Captions garble the acronym repeatedly, and every instance is corrected to NRF.

Afghanistan Freedom Front (AFF) — The shadowy guerrilla organization commanded by Yasin Zia. The narration introduces a National Liberation Front and then argues about the AFF, so the two are never merged here.

National Liberation Front (NLF) — The front as introduced, kept distinct from the AFF because the narration's mid-section switch looks like conflation, not evidence of identity.

United Front of Afghanistan (AUF) — The Kandahar-rooted, Pashtun-heavy front of Sami Sadat, presented as the sign that discontent reached the movement's own base.

Islamic State of Khorasan Province (IS-K) — The jihadist rival positioning itself as the more radical Sunni alternative. The narration's feminine pronoun for the group is kept verbatim and flagged as a slip.

Northern Alliance — The late-1990s anti-Taliban coalition, invoked as the NRF's spiritual predecessor.

Afghan National Army — The former republic's army, which the narration says stood down before overwhelming forces.

Pakistani Taliban — The separate militant movement whose attacks on Pakistan feed the cross-border war passage.

People

Ahmad Massoud — NRF leader in exile in France, son of Ahmad Shah Massoud.

Ahmad Shah Massoud — The late Lion of Panjshir, invoked as lineage rather than actor.

Kadam Shah Shahim — NRF field commander inside Afghanistan. Spelling flagged: the record form may be Qadam Shah.

Yasin Zia — AFF commander and former defense minister.

Sami Sadat — AUF leader. Spelling flagged: captions vary between Sammy and Sami, and the first form is kept until a record check settles it.

Jumakhan Feter — The Tajik Taliban commander behind the Nusai gold-mine revolt. Spelling flagged: captions vary between Feter and Fether.

Qassem Soleimani — The slain commander whose memorial bombing is attributed to IS-K.

Bilal Sarwari — The journalist cited as an on-the-ground voice on the Nangarhar resurgence. Spelling flagged: possibly Sarwary. Cited as attributed voice, never sole witness.

Places

Panjshir — The valley first claimed by the NRF, then abandoned within weeks.

Kabul — The capital and the resistance's unreachable objective.

Kandahar — The southern stronghold and the supreme leader's seat, hence the chapter's shock-barometer.

Badakhshan — The northern province of the Feter revolt, including Nusai and Zebak districts.

Nangarhar — The eastern province of the reported IS-K resurgence.

Helmand — The southern province named alongside Kandahar in the hunger passage.

Tajikistan — The neighbor named as an NRF backer.

Practices and Terms

Social contract — The course lens: authority accepted in exchange for welfare and order. The concept reading is confirmed against the cited explainer; the Taliban's breach of it is the video's thesis.

Secret police — The narration's term for the Taliban's surveillance and infiltration apparatus, central to the strategy theory.

Recognition — Diplomatic acceptance. The Russia-only line is confirmed with nuance, and every engagement short of it stays engagement, not recognition.

Sheath — The underwear brand behind the paid sponsor read. Labeled as promotion wherever it interrupts, never a source.
